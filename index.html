watch Micron's CTO publicly rebuts CXMT's 4F² claims; Taoyuan strike proceeds as scheduled

Micron CTO Scott DeBoer told Nikkei Asia (published 10/22) that Micron has studied 4F² DRAM for two decades but it is not on the company's roadmap — he called comparisons to CXMT's 4F² progress "smoke and mirrors," arguing capacitor pitch, not cell structure, determines DRAM scaling, and that 4F² is "complex and costly" with fundamental physics constraints. Micron is instead continuing 6F² plus next-generation 3D DRAM. This is the first time a US memory incumbent has gone on-record with a specific technical rebuttal of a CXMT roadmap claim, rather than the market simply reacting to share/revenue disclosures (as on 10/19) or an unconfirmed product claim (as on 10/12-13) — notable because CXMT's parallel announcement (mass-producing 5th-gen DRAM, targeting 4F² server memory by 2026) evidently registered as credible enough to warrant a public response. Separately, Micron's Taoyuan union began its previously-flagged 24-hour warning strike at 9am local time on 10/22 (ends 9am 10/23) — proceeding exactly as scheduled after the Taichung union's separate dispute was resolved 10/21; Taoyuan and Taichung together account for more than 60% of Micron's total output. No change to P015 (95%, resolves on C3Q26 contract pricing). (Nikkei Asia; Reuters via Investing.com)

watch TSMC seeks rare-earth-free factory equipment to de-risk China's export controls

Reuters (exclusive, 10/22) reports TSMC has surveyed suppliers on rare-earth-free motors for factory-automation equipment, aiming to reduce exposure to China's 2025 rare-earth export-control licensing regime (rare-earth magnets are common in motors powering moving parts in fab automation gear). Discussions remain early-stage — no broad rollout has been decided. Separately, Taiwan's government is funding research toward meeting ~50% of domestic rare-earth needs within three years, with the US also engaging Taiwan on alternative supply/processing. An early-stage, pre-emptive supply-chain-resilience move on a China-controlled physical input, not a near-term capacity change. (Japan Times; Reuters via Yahoo Finance Canada)

watch NVIDIA/Tesla reportedly commit $2.8B to Samsung Electro-Mechanics substrate lines; Korean press flags "circular financing"

Tesla and NVIDIA will reportedly invest a combined 3.7 trillion won (~$2.8B — Tesla ~1.1T won/~$0.8B, NVIDIA ~2.6T won/~$2.0B) in Samsung Electro-Mechanics to build FC-BGA chip-substrate production lines in Sejong, South Korea, under a long-term supply contract covering 2029-2033. Tesla's share is tied to substrates for its autonomous vehicles and Optimus robots; NVIDIA's larger share covers AI accelerators and data-center networking switches. None of the three companies has confirmed the reported figures. Korea's Maeil Business Newspaper framed the deal as raising "circular financing" concerns — the same structural worry previously applied to OpenAI-Nvidia-Microsoft-style compute deals, now extending into the substrate/packaging layer. Two-sided: a genuine capacity-expansion signal at a real physical bottleneck (substrates), but another instance of the largest AI compute buyers directly financing their own supply chain's capacity. No prediction impact. (US News)
