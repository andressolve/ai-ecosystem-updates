bear NVIDIA succession crisis widens: Huang comeback talk splits Silicon Valley

The Financial Times reported Sunday (11/15) that Jensen Huang has discussed a possible NVIDIA return with confidants since his 11/11 resignation, and that an ally is organizing a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby the board for his reinstatement. Elon Musk is among the signers of an open letter urging reinstatement, which had more than 176,000 signatures by Monday morning per Forbes; Musk's stated rationale is that many of Huang's own supply-chain deals were handshake agreements rather than written contracts, and could unwind without him personally driving them. Pulling the other way, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has publicly called for a "dramatic shakeup" favoring an outside CEO, and analyst Dan Ives said he sees no realistic path back for Huang. Press reports (The Information, unconfirmed) say headhunters have sounded out Arm CEO Rene Haas as one external candidate. NVIDIA has called succession speculation premature and says CFO Colette Kress continues as interim CEO; no board decision has been reported. NVDA fell further in Monday premarket (-2.70% to $158.44), part of the broader selloff described above, heading into Tuesday's (11/17, after close) Q3 FY27 earnings — consensus is roughly $108.8B revenue and $2.47 adjusted EPS. This is an escalation of the governance thread opened 11/12, not new information about demand or supply, consistent with every prior entry in this saga. Watch whether tomorrow's call, run by Kress, becomes a de facto referendum on interim leadership — the pre-set P035 test (hold ~65% if she reaffirms the ~70% FY2028 growth guide, cut toward ~50% if she withdraws or declines to repeat it) is logically separate from the comeback talk itself. (FT via Yahoo Finance; Forbes; Fox Business; CNBC)

bear Disconfirming: SemiAnalysis cuts its own 2030 AI accelerator capex forecast by ~40%

SemiAnalysis revised its Accelerator & HBM Model on 11/13, cutting its projected 2030 global AI accelerator capex from $1.531T to $923B — a 40% cut, $608B in absolute terms. The 2028 forecast fell 28% (roughly $1.05T to $758B) and 2029 fell about 41%; cumulative 2027-2030 spending was cut by roughly $1.6T. The firm attributes the revision to tighter capital markets and slower expansion among frontier AI labs, and sees NVIDIA as the most exposed supplier. The important nuance: physical volumes were cut far less than dollars — by 2030, logic silicon area falls only about 15% and HBM bit demand only about 12%, while accelerator power draw falls 34% (122.8GW to 80.7GW) — all smaller than the 40% capex cut. That gap suggests the revision leans more on lower assumed chip prices and efficiency gains than on a proportional collapse in the physical scale of the buildout, which is a genuinely two-sided read for this project's core hypothesis: cheaper, more efficient compute is part of the computational-abundance logic, even as it undercuts the dollar-capex figure this thesis has used as a demand proxy. Sourcing caveat: I could not reach SemiAnalysis's own paywalled institutional note; the figures above are consistent across three independent secondary outlets (a research Substack, PANews, Futunn) but have not been verified against the primary model.
