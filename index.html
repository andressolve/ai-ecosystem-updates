Overnight Developments

watch Anthropic files its S-1 publicly: $16.3B H1 revenue, $450-518B in disclosed compute commitments, valuation chatter cools to ~$1.5T

Anthropic's S-1 went public at 9am ET, converting the 9/29-leaked draft into an SEC-filed, audited-standard disclosure ahead of a planned mid-November IPO (roadshow marketing expected the week of 11/9). H1 2026 revenue was $16.3B (15x YoY from $1.1B); Q3 alone was $16.8B (+45% QoQ), and the company says it was adjusted-profitable in Q3. The headline H1 net loss of $80.7B is dominated (~$72B, ~89%) by a non-cash fair-value charge on convertible-note/financing-instrument liabilities — an accounting artifact of successful fundraising, not cash burn; the adjusted H1 net loss is ~$8.7B. H1 infrastructure costs were ~$9B, up 4x YoY — meaning revenue grew roughly 4x faster than infrastructure cost, a genuine unit-economics data point. The filing itemizes $450-518B in non-cancelable cloud/chip commitments over 7-10 years: Google Cloud $111B, AWS $110B, Microsoft $31B, plus separate chip commitments of $86B+ to Nvidia and $160B+ to Broadcom/Google custom silicon. On dollars, non-NVIDIA-denominated commitments (~$381B+) outweigh the explicit NVIDIA line (~$86B) by roughly 4:1 — the most concrete, SEC-filed ratio logged on this question to date, though it blends training and inference spend and stops short of an explicit "majority of training compute" statement. Amazon holds ~21% of Anthropic equity, Google ~15%; roughly one-fifth of H1 revenue came from just two customers. IPO valuation chatter has cooled to ~$1.5T (from ~$2T floated in September), still well above the ~$965B May private mark. P003 downgraded 38% → 35% ↓ on this filing. (Bloomberg; Yahoo Finance/dealroom.co summary of the S-1)

bear Musk's "guppy-level death" call and Nebius's own CEO both confirm neocloud financing has tightened — one day after Huang named capital as the binding constraint

Elon Musk said on X (10/25) that debt markets have "almost entirely" shut off for any neocloud without substantial non-AI-datacenter revenue, predicting 200+ neoclouds face "guppy-level death and consolidation" — contrasting SpaceX's own $40B investment-grade GPU debt raise (backed by Starlink/launch revenue). Nebius CEO Arkady Volozh independently corroborated the substance the same day: "From last week, probably some of the capital sources are temporarily closed, like asset-backed or debt market," though he said customer advances and other instruments should still be enough to fund Nebius's committed gigawatt build (Nebius has a $3.5B Citi facility and ~$40B of long-term contracts as potential collateral). This is the first operator-side confirmation of the capital-scarcity theme Jensen Huang raised three days ago at GTC Berlin — more probative than Huang's framing since Volozh is describing his own company's access, not a supplier's read on customers (while Musk, a competitor for GPU/capital access, has his own incentive to talk down rivals' odds). CRWV fell a further -3.46% and NBIS -0.86% in Monday premarket, on top of CoreWeave's ~-38% October decline. Logged two-sided to the thesis tracker: bearish for weak-balance-sheet neocloud survival, but Nebius's own claim that contracted-revenue-backed financing still works is a real distinction from a sector-wide freeze. (CNBC; Yahoo Finance/Benzinga on Volozh)

watch Meta raises up to $95B in equity — first stock sale since 2013 — with NVIDIA, AMD, and Broadcom among prospective buyers

Meta announced ~$35B of offerings now ($22.5B Class A common via forward-sale agreements with Morgan Stanley/Goldman, settling by November 2027, plus $12.5B of mandatory convertible preferred depositary shares) and a further ~$60B Class C at-the-market program starting Q1 2027 — its first stock issuance since 2013, explicitly to fund AI capex ($135-145B guided for 2026) alongside operating cash flow, investment-grade debt, and "strategic partnerships." NVIDIA, AMD, and Broadcom indicated non-binding interest in buying up to an aggregate $5B of the Class A offering on the same terms as other investors. META traded roughly flat (-0.39%) premarket. This lands one trading day after Meta's first-ever negative-FCF quarter (-$2.12B, reported Thursday) — a concrete instance of financing pressure reaching even the best-capitalized hyperscalers, though the calm stock reaction suggests equity markets are still receptive to funding AI capex at scale, unlike the debt/asset-backed markets Musk and Volozh described for neoclouds above. The chip-supplier interest is a new circular-financing data point one layer up the stack from last week's Samsung Electro-Mechanics item: NVIDIA/AMD/Broadcom's sales depend on Meta's capex, and now they are prospective buyers of the equity funding that capex. (Meta IR, Form FWP (SEC primary); CNBC)

neutral Samsung overtakes SK Hynix in HBM revenue (Bernstein est.); both report Wednesday alongside a shareholder-return decision

Bernstein estimates Samsung's Q3 2026 HBM revenue hit $8.7B (+33% QoQ), overtaking SK Hynix's estimated $7.6B (+11% QoQ) for the first time in the five quarters Bernstein has tracked — driven by HBM4, where Samsung says its own Q3 HBM4 revenue more than tripled QoQ and will exceed 60% of total HBM revenue in H2 2026. This is a sell-side estimate, not yet company-confirmed: both companies report actual Q3 segment results before 9am Seoul time Wednesday (10/28), when SK Hynix will also announce a shareholder-return decision (reports floated a possible ~₩16T/~$12B special dividend funded by Kioxia stake gains, which SK Hynix has not confirmed). Doesn't move P015 (95%, resolves on contract pricing, not share). (Investing.com/Bernstein; TipRanks on SK Hynix earnings date)

bull AWS CEO: cloud capacity sold out through 2027, "a good chunk" of 2028 already committed; Verizon signs $2B+ AI deal

On an a16z podcast published 10/23 (surfaced in today's sweep), AWS CEO Andy Jassy said 2026 capacity is fully sold out, 2027 is "largely sold out," and "a good chunk" of 2028 is already committed, leaving only reserves for on-demand EC2/Bedrock inference; he pegged 2026 capex at ~$220B and called AI-driven demand "insatiable," saying AWS "could invest a lot more and it wouldn't be a bad use of capital." Separately, Verizon signed a $2B+ AI-infrastructure deal with AWS and raised its own annual forecast on the back of it. This is the clearest capacity-constraint confirmation from a hyperscaler CEO logged since Huang's GTC Berlin remark three days earlier — demand-side, not financing-side, reinforcing that this week's capex debate (Meta's raise, neocloud financing stress) is about funding mechanism and ROI timing, not whether the underlying compute is wanted. No P008 impact (demand confirmation, not new capex guidance). (aiweekly.co; Reuters on Verizon-AWS)
