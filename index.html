bear CXMT discloses Q3 revenue surge of 589-619% YoY, global DRAM share doubles to 10%

China's CXMT (Shanghai STAR Market-listed) forecast first-nine-months 2026 revenue of ~RMB 265-270B (vs. RMB 32.1B a year earlier), with Q3 alone projected at RMB 114.7-119.7B (+589-619% YoY), attributing the surge to AI-driven DRAM demand and pricing. Its global DRAM revenue share rose to 10% in Q3 2026 from 5% a year earlier. CXMT's own shares nonetheless fell ~7.8% on the print (profit-taking after a well-telegraphed number), and Samsung (+1.16%)/SK Hynix (+0.39%) continued their rebound in Seoul rather than selling off on the news. This is the highest-quality evidence yet for the memory-moat competitive-threat axis first logged 10/12 — a company-disclosed revenue filing, not a product-roadmap claim — though it's two-sided by construction: CXMT attributes its own boom to the same AI-DRAM demand this tracker treats as supporting evidence for the core thesis, even as it confirms a real, quantified share shift away from Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron in server-grade DRAM. Doesn't test P015 (95%, resolves on C3Q26 contract pricing, not competitor share). Logged Strong to theses/computational-abundance.md. (CNBC; Bloomberg; Seoul Economic Daily)

watch CoreWeave: Goldman Sachs upgrades to Buy, directly rebutting the financing-cost fear, as CDS holds at Friday's record

Goldman Sachs upgraded CoreWeave to Buy ($106 PT, from Neutral), citing stronger contract economics from the Meta deal (ARR/MW, IRR outlook) and arguing investors are "underestimating return improvement while overestimating financing-cost increases" — the firm's own model expects CoreWeave's blended cost of debt to rise only 100-200bps, and separately notes its >15% unsecured bond yields may overstate its true marginal financing cost given reliance on asset-backed debt. CRWV +6.01% premarket, NBIS +6.94% in sympathy. This is the first detailed bank model to directly rebut this axis's core financing-cost fear with bottom-up analysis rather than reassurance rhetoric (cf. NVIDIA's Huang, 10/16) — but Goldman is sell-side with underwriting ties to the sector, and CoreWeave's 5yr CDS remained at Friday's record ~1,190bps/>60% implied default odds as of this morning, i.e., the credit market hasn't followed. Logged Moderate, two-sided, to theses/computational-abundance.md and P008's context notes (held 63%). (Seeking Alpha; Investing.com)

bull Nvidia standardizes next-gen custom HBM base dies on TSMC's 3nm process across Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron

Nvidia asked Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron to use TSMC's 3nm process for the base dies of its next-gen custom HBM (NVHBM, unveiled August 2026), which integrates Nvidia's own memory controller to standardize implementation across suppliers. Samsung accepted after an internal review — the first time it has worked with TSMC at the HBM manufacturing stage — while keeping its in-house model for standard HBM and other customers' products. This extends TSMC's physical-layer footprint beyond logic and CoWoS packaging into a standardized role inside the memory stack itself, though it's base-die logic (TSMC's core competency) rather than DRAM capacity, so it doesn't bear on the memory suppliers' own capacity constraints or add TSMC revenue at memory-industry scale. Logged Moderate supporting. (Reuters via TradingView; Yahoo Finance)

watch Microsoft exits planned UK data center as local grid can't deliver power until the early-to-mid 2030s

Microsoft walked away from a planned Nscale-built, 50MW/23,040-Nvidia-GPU AI data center in Loughton, Essex (UK), meant to supply Azure capacity by early 2027, after UK Power Networks told Nscale the local grid may not have capacity until the early-to-mid 2030s — a decade-long delay that made the original plan unviable. Nscale (a Bitcoin-mining offshoot that filed for a US IPO last month, with Microsoft and Anthropic expected as major customers) says its other Microsoft contracts remain in place and it's pursuing alternative power sources for the site. This is a concrete, quantified instance of the power-constraint falsification axis actually canceling a specific, named, dated deployment — distinct from the aggregate GW-level power deals (e.g., Google-Constellation, 10/6) logged as supporting evidence; it cuts the other way, showing grid capacity can bind hard enough to kill a committed project outright. No prediction impact (not a Big-5 capex guidance change). Logged to the power-constraint axis. (CoinDesk; Benzinga)

neutral Anthropic pledges 5% of equity to a new public-benefit fund ahead of reported November IPO; Musk responds with a SpaceX share-per-child pledge

Anthropic announced plans (subject to shareholder approval) to commit 5% of its shares to a new "Anthropic Public Benefit Fund," with the board able to add 2% more for every 50% increase in valuation above a set baseline, to "explore large donations" and study/address AI-driven job disruption — on top of $200M already committed to related research. This is a governance/political move, not a compute-demand data point — but it lands the same weekend Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei renewed his "pace the frontier" regulatory call in a fresh interview, and ahead of a reported November IPO where equity structure will matter to valuation optics. No prediction impact (doesn't test P037, which resolves on a disclosed compute/capex cut, not governance commitments). (CNBC; Reuters; Benzinga)
