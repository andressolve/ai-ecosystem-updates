Overnight Developments

bear Nasdaq's worst day since April 2025 crystallizes the political/data-center-opposition axis into a quantified, real repricing

The Nasdaq fell -5.5% on 11/5 (S&P 500 -4.4%), its worst session since April 2025, erasing roughly $3T in S&P 500 market value. NVDA fell -9.26% to $181.60 (~$440-450B lost, its worst day since the January 2025 DeepSeek shock); AMD and Micron were down as much as 16% intraday; Qualcomm fell ~9-11% after guiding Q1 EPS below Street on weak handset sales, adding a company-specific trigger on top of the political story. The proximate cause, per wire coverage, was a delayed market reaction to this week's midterm results (Democratic sweep, Texas/Georgia/Ohio governors-elect on explicit data-center-moratorium platforms) compounding with bubble-valuation concerns. Two new figures quantify the axis for the first time: Citi Research's tracker of US data-center bans/pauses went from near zero through mid-2025 to ~20 by December 2025, over 300 by June 2026, and over 500 by October 2026; UBS separately estimates ~30% of expected 2028 US data-center capacity additions are at risk from construction pauses. That 30% figure is in tension with the SemiAnalysis finding (logged 11/5) that only ~4% (1.5 of 38 GW) of 2027 capacity was actually delayed as of September — different years and different methodologies (realized delay vs. forward-looking risk estimate), not necessarily contradictory, but not yet reconciled either. (CNBC, Fortune, Benzinga/Citi)

watch Asian chip stocks open near limit-down, then recover sharply into their local closes; US premarket is bouncing hard

SK Hynix closed Seoul's 11/6 session -10.44%, Samsung -6.35% (both reportedly deeper intraday — SK Hynix as low as -11.7%); Taiwan's TAIEX fell as much as ~9% intraday (its largest-ever point drop) before closing -4.51%, and TSMC (2330.TW) closed -4.69% after opening near its 10% limit-down band. In US premarket this morning, the same names are sharply higher: NVDA +4.08%, AVGO +4.59%, TSM ADR +7.00%, Micron +7.45%, Arista +5.65%, CoreWeave +9.94%, Nebius +9.66%. CNBC's Jim Cramer argued NVDA's multiple is "the cheapest...in about a decade"; there's active debate over whether NVIDIA should deploy its $235B buyback authorization at current levels. Former Goldman Sachs CEO Lloyd Blankfein called the moratorium "hysteria" a "gift to China" the same day. On CNBC, Niles Investment Management's Dan Niles took the opposite side, warning most neocloud providers (naming CoreWeave and Nebius) could go to zero on overbuilding, debt, and weak cash flow, and said he doesn't think Thursday marked the cycle's peak. (24/7 Wall St., Benzinga/Niles)

watch Congressional Republicans request a closed-door briefing from Jensen Huang over alleged ties to a Chinese intelligence asset

Three House Republican committee chairs (Foreign Affairs' Brian Mast, the China Select Committee's John Moolenaar, and Intelligence's Rick Crawford) made public a letter requesting Jensen Huang brief Congress on 11/18 — the day after NVDA's 11/17 Q3 FY27 earnings — about a New York Times report that U.S. intelligence identified Chinese national and Megaspeed executive Alice Huang as funneling information to Beijing and found she had "developed a close relationship" with him. Trump was reportedly briefed on the underlying intelligence roughly a year ago and took no action. NVIDIA says the contacts involved only "ordinary business information," not trade secrets or anything tied to US national security. This is a new, NVDA-specific political/regulatory risk, distinct from the data-center-moratorium axis, landing one day after earnings and worth tracking through the briefing date. (CNBC)
