Overnight Developments

watch NVIDIA's board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO, reversing the November 11 resignation

NVIDIA's own newsroom confirms the board voted unanimously to reinstate Huang as President, CEO and board director, effective immediately, on November 23 — less than two weeks after accepting his resignation and six days after Chair Harvey Jones told the Q3 FY27 earnings call the board did not intend to reinstate him. The board said it was "satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor and ability to lead NVIDIA" and received input from "senior levels of the United States government," concluding reinstatement poses no national security risk. Colette Kress returns to CFO only; Jones remains chair. PBS reports Trump called for Huang's return as recently as Sunday night and took credit for the board's decision — the administration itself appears to have pushed in the opposite direction from Treasury Secretary Bessent's prior public preference for an outside hire, a tension worth watching into Bessent's self-set ~November 25 deadline for the board to answer export-control questions. NVIDIA says its independent-director committee investigating the alleged foreign-intelligence-targeting of Huang continues, as does the National Security and Export Control Committee's work and the search for a Chief Compliance Officer; the SEC civil probe, the California AG inquiry, and the December Senate Banking hearings (Huang 12/3, Lutnick 12/10) are all unaffected.

This is NVIDIA-specific governance resolution, not AI compute-demand evidence, consistent with how the original resignation was logged — but it directly resolves P042 YES, and it exposes a real calibration miss: the companion identity-distribution forecast set on 11/19 assigned only a 5% weight to "Huang returns," when a board's public, on-the-record denial six days earlier turned out to be weak evidence once the person denying it came under direct presidential pressure to reverse. NVDA gained roughly 31% Monday (its best single day since May 2003, ~$1T of market cap), and the reaction propagated through the stack: TSMC's Taipei shares had their best day since 2008 (+9.85%, about $158B of value in one session), with SK Hynix (+6.73%) and Samsung (+6.70%) also rallying sharply in Seoul.
