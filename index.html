Overnight Developments

bear Democrats flip Texas and Georgia governorships on explicit data-center-moratorium platforms

Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in the November 3 midterms. In the two most consequential state races for this thesis, Gina Hinojosa defeated incumbent Greg Abbott for Texas governor (52.9%–45.6%), having campaigned explicitly on a moratorium on new data-center construction, an end to related water/power tax breaks, and stricter water/power-usage regulation. Texas alone is commonly cited as accounting for more than 25% of planned US data-center capacity through 2030. Keisha Lance Bottoms won the Georgia governorship, ending two decades of Republican control in the state where Atlanta is the most active data-center construction market in the country.

This is the clearest escalation yet of a thread this file has tracked since May 27 (the Pope/Sanders-bill/14-state-moratorium row, logged Weak-Moderate) through outgoing Gov. Abbott's own administrative ERCOT/TCEQ permitting pauses (August 3, September 21-22, logged Moderate-Strong). Those were a sitting governor pausing approvals pending an audit; this is a successor elected on an explicit promise to stop new construction outright. Because Abbott already demonstrated the mechanism — a direct agency order, not legislation — Hinojosa likely has the same administrative lever available immediately upon taking office in January 2027, which is why this is logged Strong on the thesis tracker rather than treated as mere campaign rhetoric. Separately, the FTC escalated its AI-safety probe of Anthropic and OpenAI from document requests (first reported October 15) to formal subpoenas and compelled executive testimony — a distinct regulatory axis (consumer-protection/agent-safety, not data-center siting), but a second same-week signal that a Democratic-controlled Washington raises the odds of broader AI regulatory scrutiny.

Market reaction split cleanly along this line: broad indices were mixed-to-higher on a divided-government/rate-path read, while AI-datacenter-exposed names sold off — the Goldman Sachs Broad AI basket (already down >24% from its October peak) fell further, and our own premarket quotes show CoreWeave -9.32% and Nebius -7.21%, larger than the ~1-3% several outlets cited in earlier premarket snapshots, suggesting the move built through the morning.

No Tier 1 hyperscaler has yet cited this specific outcome as a 2027 capex risk. New prediction P041 tracks whether Hinojosa (or the Texas legislature) actually enacts a binding administrative or statutory pause before 2027-12-31 — logged at a genuine-guess 50%, since no comparable base rate exists.

bull Arista's fastest growth since IPO and Apple's "unprecedented" memory warning confirm real demand underneath the financing-fragility story

Arista reported Q3 2026 revenue of $3.66B (+58.5% YoY, the highest growth rate since its 2014 IPO), beating its own $3.3B guide, and raised FY2026 guidance to $13.3B (+48%) with an initial 2027 framing of ~$18.5B (+38-40%) — a clean, audited demand print landing the same morning the broader tape is selling AI-infrastructure names on political and financing-fragility fears. Stock +3.58% premarket.

Separately, Apple CEO John Ternus told Apple's Q4 2026 earnings call that memory pricing is "unprecedented," said Apple paid more for memory in September than in June, and expects costs to keep rising into and beyond the December quarter — guiding Apple's own gross margin down to 45-46% partly as a result. TrendForce responded by raising its Q4 2026 contract-price forecasts to +10-15% QoQ for conventional DRAM and +15-20% QoQ for NAND. A buyer with every incentive to talk prices down calling the environment "unprecedented" is a stronger, independent confirmation of memory pricing power than supplier commentary alone. Reinforces P015 (95%, at ceiling) and P028 (55%).

watch SK Hynix's New York fab stalls on a US-China export-license holdup

Reuters reports SK Hynix's planned $45B New York NAND plant is stalled because the US Commerce Department has not approved export licenses the company needs to equip its Dalian, China facility — SK Hynix argues increased Dalian output would help fund the New York buildout, but Commerce revoked standing authorizations for foreign chipmakers in China in 2025 and replaced them with annual, quantity-limited licenses still pending approval. This is a genuine US-China export-control collision with SK Hynix's own US capacity-expansion plans; it does not bear on near-term DRAM contract pricing (P015 unaffected) but is a new friction point on the 2027+ supply-response timeline this file has tracked since the June ADR/Yongin entries.
