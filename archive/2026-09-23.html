Overnight Developments

[bear/watch] Micron's Taiwan union moves from failed mediation to a formal strike vote

The Taoyuan union announced (per Digitimes and Reuters, 9/21-22) it will hold a formal strike vote in early October after its second mediation session with Micron (9/21) ended without agreement — extending the dispute first logged 9/7 and 9/21. Micron has offered no new counter-proposal; management said a bonus plan exists but can't be disclosed until board approval in early October, tied to the FY26 earnings cycle. The union rejected a NT$1M (~$31,500) one-time bonus Micron announced unilaterally on 9/11. A separate Taichung-site mediation is now scheduled for 10/22. No source quantifies a production-impact estimate yet — treat any capacity-loss figure as speculation. This is a genuine escalation (vote announced, not just a second failed session) but still one procedural step short of an actual strike date. It lands nine days before Micron's own FQ4 FY26 earnings (Wed 9/30, AMC) — the print will be the first chance for management to address it directly. No impact to P015 (95%)/P028 (55%)/P029 (53%); same supply-side execution-risk read as prior entries, now with a firmer timeline. Logged to watchlist/tier2/micron.md.

[bull/watch] Nebius raises GPU rental prices again — a direct test of yesterday's Redburn bear case

Nebius confirmed (Reuters, first reported 9/17, corroborated by Barchart 9/22) a second round of GPU rental price increases in three months, effective October 1: H100 $3.85→$4.50 (+17%), H200 $4.50→$5.40 (+20%), B200 $7.15→$8.50 (+19%), B300 $7.85→$9.50 (+21%), AMD/Intel CPU instances +25%, and memory-attached instances +41% — the steepest of any category. This follows a May 2026 hike (+29% on-demand average, +51% spot). This is a direct, company-sourced rebuttal to Rothschild Redburn's 9/21 Sell initiation on both CoreWeave and Nebius, which cited falling GPU rental prices as a core thesis leg. It does not, on its own, resolve the question for CoreWeave — no CoreWeave-specific rental-pricing data was confirmed this sweep, and CRWV's own July SKU price increases (~25%, disclosed on its Q2 print) are the closest analog but predate Redburn's call. Worth a direct pricing-index check (cross-ref reference_gpu_pricing_sources.md — SemiAnalysis, Silicon Data SDH100RT) in a future session to see whether market-wide GPU rental pricing is actually falling, as Redburn assumed, or whether Redburn's thesis was already stale at publication. Logged to watchlist/tier2/nebius.md.

Anthropic's DRAM demand estimated at 40B units by 2027 — analyst estimate, not company-confirmed

Korean trade press (Economy Tribune, DigiTimes, 9/22-23) reported that Anthropic's projected 2027 DRAM demand could reach "40-50 billion" 1Gb-equivalent units — a scale said to rival NVIDIA's own DRAM consumption on the same normalized metric — with roughly 20% purchased directly from memory makers rather than through server OEMs. The figure traces to a September 15 Edgewater Research report relayed through the Korean press chain; it is not confirmed by Anthropic, Micron, Samsung, or SK Hynix, and no Reuters/Bloomberg/earnings-call corroboration was found. What is independently confirmed: Micron signed a supply agreement with Anthropic in June 2026, and Samsung/SK Hynix both took strategic equity stakes in Anthropic's ~$965B round, with memory-supply terms "discussed" per Amodei's remarks at the Korea AI summit but undisclosed. Flagging as a lead to verify against the next round of Micron/Samsung/SK Hynix earnings calls, not as evidence — consistent with house style on labeling estimates as estimates. If even directionally correct, it would be a meaningful new demand-side data point for the memory-undersupply thesis (frontier labs as direct DRAM buyers, not just GPU/server buyers).
