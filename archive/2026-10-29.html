Overnight Developments

bear Hyperscaler capex guidance pauses for the first time this cycle, even as cloud demand accelerates

Alphabet reported Q3 2026 revenue of $127.6B (+25% YoY) after the close on 10/28. Google Cloud revenue hit $30.7B (+103% YoY, accelerating from Q2's 82%), operating margin expanded to 36.2% from 23.7% a year ago, and backlog grew to $561B (55%+ expected to convert to revenue within 24 months). GAAP EPS of $2.12 missed the $3.01 consensus by ~30% — but the gap is almost entirely an $8.2B unrealized mark-to-market loss on Alphabet's SpaceX equity stake; adjusted EPS excluding the item was $2.67 versus a $3.02 adjusted consensus. Capex was $51.4B in Q3; full-year guidance held unchanged at $195-205B, the first quarter this year Alphabet has not raised it (prior path: $175-185B → $180-190B → $195-205B).

Hours earlier, Microsoft posted FY27 Q1 revenue of $92.3B (+19% YoY), with Azure revenue up 48% to $33.2B — a re-acceleration, not a slowdown — and adjusted EPS of $4.89 beating the $4.73 consensus. Capex was $45.2B (+30% YoY), and FY27 full-year capex was guided to approximately $175B, a figure the Street had reportedly modeled higher, which drove a "capex miss" narrative despite the underlying beat.

GOOG fell 11.51% and MSFT rose a muted 1.88% in premarket trading. More notable for the broader ecosystem: CoreWeave (-5.90%) and Nebius (-5.94%) sold off on the read that hyperscaler capex not being raised again reduces the odds of GPU-overflow demand reaching neoclouds — compounding the financing-fragility axis already under pressure since Elon Musk's "guppy-level death" comments on 10/26.

Demand-side metrics from both companies are unambiguously strong and still accelerating; what paused is the rate of upward guidance revision, not the spending level itself — both guides remain at historically unprecedented absolute dollars. One data point from two companies is not yet a trend. Amazon reports tonight (10/29, after the close) as the next test of whether this is a two-company pause or a broader one.

bull Samsung's Q3 results independently confirm SK Hynix's memory-shortage story

Samsung reported Q3 2026 revenue of ₩195.3T (+127% YoY) and operating profit of ₩107.4T (+783% YoY) — the first tech company ever to report a quarterly operating profit above ₩100T. Memory revenue rose 427% YoY to ₩140.7T; HBM4 sales more than tripled quarter-over-quarter and now make up 60% of total HBM revenue, and Samsung says its 2027 HBM supply volume is already sold out at agreed pricing. Net profit (₩75.1T) missed consensus (₩89.06T) on continued losses in the mobile division, and the stock fell 4.13% in Seoul despite the headline beat — a reminder that Samsung's conglomerate structure means its share price doesn't transmit the memory story as cleanly as SK Hynix's does (SK Hynix closed +2.62% today in continued follow-through on its own 10/28 print).

This is the second independent primary-source confirmation in two days of the DRAM/HBM undersupply thesis, this time from the #2 HBM supplier with no incentive to coordinate messaging with its rival. P015 (already at its 95% ceiling) gets a second reinforcing data point with no room to move further.

watch NVIDIA faces a second live DOJ antitrust inquiry

Reuters reported (10/28) that the DOJ is examining whether NVIDIA's pending $12.9B acquisition of Hugging Face would hand it a "foundational point of distribution for AI" — a second concurrent antitrust inquiry alongside the Groq-licensing probe opened in September. NVDA closed -1.17% at $215.38 on the news. The deal (announced September, expected to close H1 2027) is not reported as blocked, only under inquiry; the likeliest outcome, consistent with the Groq-probe precedent, remains a fine or conduct remedy rather than an unwind. Separately, AMD confirmed a $1B commitment to Meta's equity raise, completing the $4B combined NVIDIA+AMD commitment to Meta's AI-infrastructure financing round first flagged 10/26.

neutral FOMC holds rates, two dissents favor a hike

The Fed held its target range at 3.75-4.00% on 10/28, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissenting in favor of a 25bp hike — a hawkish dissent, not a dovish one. Chair Warsh upgraded the economic-activity assessment from "solid" to "strong," framed the hold as an "observational pause" to evaluate the transmission of September's hike, and declined to give forward guidance for December.
