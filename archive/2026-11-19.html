watch BIS export rule starts biting: TSMC, Samsung and NVIDIA all curtail China shipments

The Financial Times reported (citing people briefed on the matter) that TSMC and Samsung have told Chinese customers in recent days that they will hold certain chips pending new US export licenses required by the 11/16 BIS rule that closed the remote-access/rental loophole (logged in theses/computational-abundance.md 11-16). Reuters independently corroborated and added detail: TSMC is also suspending some previously-permitted advanced-manufacturing orders and generally limiting affected China customers to older process nodes. Reported affected TSMC customers include smartphone-chip clients Xiaomi, Oppo and Vivo — notably broader than pure AI accelerators, extending the rule's practical reach into general advanced-node China demand. Separately, NVIDIA itself reportedly told customers it will stop shipping some licensed Hopper-class chips to China (CBS News), following a reported $2.4B quarter of China Hopper revenue disclosed at last week's earnings. This is the first concrete, multi-company implementation record behind a rule that was, as of 11/16, just a published text with a 90-day grace window. It doesn't change the thesis's core read: Chinese AI-accelerator access has been curtailed by export rules since 2022-2023, and none of TSMC's, Samsung's, or NVIDIA's guidance depends materially on it. 2330.TW closed Taiwan -3.45% on the news; TSM ADR +0.73% in US premarket (the ADR's premarket move does not reflect the Taiwan session's reaction — treat the Taiwan close as the more complete market read).
