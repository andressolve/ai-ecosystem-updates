Overnight Developments

bear NVIDIA confirms a real chip residual-value guarantee — up to 25%, per project

Jensen Huang, appearing on CNBC's "Closing Bell: Overtime" with Becky Quick alongside the six financing-platform partners (Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, KKR), clarified that NVIDIA will guarantee up to 25% of the residual value of its chips in individual financing deals, reviewed on a project-by-project basis — not an aggregate platform-wide commitment. Huang characterized the exposure as "significantly lower" than comparable compute-financing arrangements, an unverified claim rather than an independent quantification. NVDA fell ~1.4% (~$70B of market cap) on the disclosure.

This directly updates yesterday's report, which characterized the $500B MOU platform as carrying "no disclosed NVIDIA guarantee obligation" — that characterization no longer holds. The gap between NVIDIA's structure and Broadcom's (BofA quantified Broadcom's AI XPU Platform exposure at $370B max/$42B max loss at full 20GW scale by 2029, the basis for BofA's 8/11 downgrade) has narrowed: NVIDIA now discloses a real contingent liability, just a narrower, per-project one rather than a blanket platform-level backstop. No aggregate dollar figure has been disclosed for NVIDIA's structure, so a direct apples-to-apples comparison to the Broadcom figure isn't yet possible. No prediction impact — P020 (AVGO FY27 AI, 92%) and P024 (AVGO-OpenAI Phase 1, 95%) are Broadcom-specific and unaffected. Logged to theses/computational-abundance.md sub-thesis #5.
