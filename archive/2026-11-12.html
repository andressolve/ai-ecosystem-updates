What Happened

bear Jensen Huang resigns as NVIDIA CEO; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO

NVIDIA's 8-K says the board accepted Huang's resignation as president, CEO and director on 11/11, effective immediately, and gives no reason. Kress (CFO since 2013) becomes interim CEO and stays CFO. Harvey Jones, a director since 1993, becomes the first-ever board chair. The board will consider internal and external successors.

How it unfolded:

  • 11/5: House committee chairs requested a briefing over a NYT report linking Huang to Alice Huang, an alleged Chinese intelligence asset.
  • 11/8: The board opened an independent investigation, saying it had "no reason to believe" national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised. Huang admitted infidelity in a letter to staff.
  • 11/9–11/10: The Information reported that Alice Huang's network bought about $7.5B of NVIDIA chips, and that firms linked to her invested in DeepSeek with a promise of Blackwell supply. The size of that investment and whether the supply was delivered are unconfirmed.
  • 11/11: The NYT reported a relationship with a direct report.

The stock fell as much as 14% at the open today and is at $165.36 (-4.82%) at midday. Bernstein halved its target to $200; Citi cut to $225 but said it sees AI demand holding.

What this changes:

  • Near-term revenue: little. Q3 earnings stay on 11/17, with Kress on the call. The constraint on NVIDIA's revenue is CoWoS and HBM supply, and the CEO change does not alter either.
  • Export policy: a lot. Huang personally won the H20 and H200 China exemptions. That advocacy is gone just as Commerce finalises a rule treating remote cloud access to Blackwell-class chips as an export, covering data centers in seven more countries including Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand. Beijing has already warned Washington against using the scandal to tighten controls. Treat NVIDIA's China upside as close to zero.
  • After 2027: more uncertain. Huang set the one-year product cadence, so product and strategy risk beyond the current roadmap rises.

The rotation into Broadcom (+5.9%) and AMD shows the market treating this as a reason to own NVIDIA's competitors, not as a reason to own less AI.

At midday the stock is 18.6 times four times Q2 FY27 adjusted EPS. That is an annualised trailing figure, not a forward P/E. (8-K, board statement 11/8, CEO transition release, TechCrunch timeline, CNBC analyst reactions, Forbes on the Commerce rule)

bear Disconfirming: Altman tells Samsung that AI demand is "double-counted"; OpenAI raises half its target

Korea's Financial News reported on 11/11, as an exclusive, that Altman told Samsung executives:

  • "There has been double-counting of demand."
  • "Only two companies, OpenAI and Anthropic, are actually driving major demand growth, so there could be a supply shock and an unsettled period."

He also said that over the long term AI use will surge and chips and data centers will be in short supply. On 11/12, CNBC reported that OpenAI closed its round at $15B and a $1.25T valuation, against an original goal of $30B at $1.4T.

Why this matters more than the NVIDIA news: the hypothesis claims measured compute demand is too low. The person running the largest single source of that demand says that, in the near term, it is too high, because the same lab workloads appear as orders at several neoclouds and suppliers at once. The funding shortfall is the first hard sign that OpenAI cannot finance itself at the pace it planned.

Reasons for caution:

  • It is a single newspaper's account of a private meeting, and OpenAI has not confirmed it.
  • Altman was speaking to a memory supplier, where talking demand down helps on price.
  • His long-term view is explicitly bullish.

What would settle it: NVIDIA's guidance on 11/17, TSMC's November revenue on 12/10, and whether any neocloud reports a cancelled contract rather than a delayed one. No probability changed yet. (Financial News, Digitimes, OpenAI round, via CNBC report)

bull TSMC posts a record October: NT$586.89B, up 59.7% from a year earlier

October revenue rose 14.7% from September and beat August's previous record by 14%. January–October revenue is up 43.3%. At roughly 31.9 NT$ per US$ (my estimate), October is about $18.4B. That is about 35% of the midpoint of TSMC's fourth-quarter guidance with two months still to come.

This is the hardest physical-layer evidence against a near-term demand gap, and it arrived in the same week as the double-counting claim. It shows wafers and packaging are still being bought at an accelerating rate. It does not show who ultimately pays for the finished systems.

TSMC's board also approved up to $500M of Anthropic IPO shares, up to $500M of OpenAI preferred stock, and a new chip investment company of up to $500M. That makes TSMC another supplier taking equity in its largest customers. P031 raised from 70% to 92%. (TSMC, SEC 6-K, TSMC board 6-K)

bull CoreWeave beats, raises, and shifts its funding to customer prepayments

Q3 (reported 11/10 after the close):

  • Revenue $3.66B, up 168%.
  • Adjusted operating income $379M, against guidance of $200–260M.
  • Backlog about $133B, with demand "far ahead" of capacity.
  • 2026 revenue guidance raised to $13.0–13.3B; expected annualised revenue of $20–21B on exit from 2026.

On funding, management said it will issue no new parent-level unsecured debt through 2027 and expects about $9B of customer prepayments and deposits. 2027 capex could reach about $66B for about 1.5 GW of new capacity, with more than 75% of committed capex tied to investment-grade and enterprise customers. The stock rose 24.7% on 11/11, its best day since April 2025.

Bull view: prepayments directly answer the "stuck mid-build" risk in our 11/3 CoreWeave discussion, because customers now fund the build.

Bear view: recourse debt still rose about $10B in the quarter. Prepayments shift risk onto customers, and the largest customer is OpenAI, whose round just came in at half its target. Microsoft is also reviewing its neocloud suppliers, CoreWeave among them. (CNBC, transcript, Tech Times)

bear Nebius beats on revenue but cuts guidance because data centers are late; stock -17.5%

Q3 results:

  • Revenue $980.8M, up 571%, against a consensus of about $906M.
  • Adjusted EBITDA $465.8M.
  • Deferred revenue $9.4B, a sign of prepayments.

Guidance changes:

  • 2026 revenue cut to $3.0–3.2B, from $3.0–3.4B.
  • 2026 capex raised to about $27B, from $20–25B.
  • The $7–9B annualised-revenue milestone moved from December to February 2027, and annualised revenue is no longer a headline KPI.
  • 2027 guidance: $12–13B revenue on $40–45B capex, mostly funded by customer prepayments.

The cause is delays at third-party data-center sites, including one in New Jersey contracted to Microsoft. That is a delivery problem: the revenue moves into 2027 rather than disappearing. But Nebius bears the cost (capex up, revenue down) and is giving investors less visibility. P039 (FY2027 revenue ≥$8B) is unchanged at 75%, since the 2027 guidance is well above that bar. (6-K, Reuters, Blockspace)

watch Microsoft cuts back on neoclouds and winds down its Oracle rental deal

  • Neocloud roster: CNBC reported on 11/6 that Microsoft plans to work with fewer neoclouds as it prioritises its own data centers. It is reviewing more than $80B of outside rental commitments.
  • Oracle: Bloomberg reported Microsoft is winding down a 2023 deal to rent Oracle capacity that runs older NVIDIA chips.
  • IREN: a networking upgrade Microsoft requested at IREN's Childress, Texas site pushed about $5B of planned Microsoft spending into January or February.

This is in-sourcing plus delay, not lower total spending. Microsoft guided about $170B of calendar-2026 spending. Even so, it squeezes how much value neoclouds can capture, and it is where double-counted demand would show up first. (CNBC, Bloomberg)

Thesis Status

The hypothesis is still active, but this week opened the first serious crack on the demand-measurement side rather than the financing side. Until now, the disconfirming evidence was about who pays (neocloud credit, OpenAI's funding) and where capacity can be built (moratoriums). Altman's remark, if accurately reported, challenges the measurement itself: summing backlogs across the stack may count the same two customers several times.

The physical layer disagrees for now: TSMC's October and CoreWeave's backlog both point up. The next three weeks are the test:

  • NVIDIA's guidance on 11/17.
  • Whether Anthropic's IPO clears on 11/18.
  • TSMC's November revenue on 12/10.
