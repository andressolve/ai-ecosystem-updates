Overnight Developments

watch NVIDIA invests $3.5B in MediaTek, brings it into the NVLink Fusion ecosystem

NVIDIA and MediaTek announced (8/31, joint press release + SEC Form FWP) an expanded partnership: NVIDIA will invest $3.5B in convertible bonds issued by MediaTek (conversion price and dilution terms not disclosed), and MediaTek will adopt NVIDIA's NVLink Fusion platform — bundling the NVLink Fusion chiplet, NVLink-C2C interconnect, and NVHBM memory integration — as a design foundation for hyperscaler and cloud-provider custom XPUs that plug into NVIDIA's rack-scale MGX/NVLink architecture. The partnership also spans NVIDIA RTX Spark/DGX Spark edge chips and MediaTek's Dimensity Auto platforms.

MediaTek is a top-tier fabless design house (Dimensity smartphone SoCs, already a TSMC customer, already doing NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 supply-chain work) — exactly the kind of firm that could otherwise emerge as an independent merchant ASIC threat alongside Broadcom and Marvell. Instead, NVIDIA is extending its own interconnect standard into MediaTek's design capability: even a hyperscaler's "custom" XPU built with MediaTek would run on NVIDIA IP (NVLink Fusion, NVHBM), preserving NVIDIA's take even where the compute die itself isn't NVIDIA's. Two-sided for sub-thesis #4 (custom silicon): bullish for NVIDIA's ecosystem-control moat extending into the custom-silicon layer; a modest complication for the "custom silicon displaces NVIDIA" framing behind AVGO/MRVL bull cases, since NVLink Fusion licensing revenue would flow to NVIDIA regardless of whose logic die wins. Logged to theses/computational-abundance.md (sub-thesis #4). No prediction impact — doesn't resolve any current P0xx cleanly.

watch SK Hynix's Japan (Miyagi) fab weighing advances, still non-final

Bloomberg and Japan Times (8/31) report SK Hynix is studying a joint-venture feasibility for a memory fab at a Miyagi Prefecture site, aimed at HBM and NAND capacity for AI demand, with investment potentially reaching "tens of trillions of won" (order of magnitude: tens of billions of USD, same scale as SK Hynix's ~$39B domestic DRAM/NAND fab program). SK Hynix explicitly says no decision is final; internal review ongoing, Chairman Chey Tae-won reportedly to visit the site personally. This is a direct follow-on to the open item flagged 8/21 (Japan fab consideration) and comes two days after SK Hynix's Indiana groundbreaking (8/27, packaging/R&D only). No new supporting-tracker entry — this is incremental process detail on an already-logged watch item, not new evidence of a decision. Watch for a final investment decision or denial.

watch CXMT starts LPDDR6 mass production, reportedly ahead of Samsung/SK Hynix

ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) announced (8/29-30, multiple outlets corroborating) it has moved LPDDR6 mobile DRAM past sampling into volume production, with Xiaomi's upcoming 18 Fold (September launch) as first customer — less than a year after CXMT's LPDDR5 mass production. This is mobile DRAM, not the server DDR5/HBM that the memory-undersupply predictions (P015/P028/P029) are keyed to, so no direct read-through to those. But it is a genuine data point that CXMT's process cadence on a leading-edge DRAM standard is now competitive with, or ahead of, Samsung and SK Hynix's public timelines — relevant context for open item #11 (unconfirmed second CXMT Beijing fab) and the broader question of how durable the Korean/US memory oligopoly's technology lead is. Two-sided, Moderate: doesn't move near-term HBM pricing power, but narrows the assumed multi-year gap between Chinese and Korean/US memory technology. No tracker entry added (mobile DRAM is outside the sub-theses this tracker covers); flagging as a watch item only.
