Qualcomm announced (9/8) that Amazon could purchase up to $60B of Qualcomm AI data-center chips and related products under a long-term partnership covering "multiple generations of customized silicon" for AWS inference infrastructure, plus high-performance optical connectivity up to 1.6 Tbps. watch OpenAI claims Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize solution — unverified, credit dispute attached

OpenAI published (9/8-9/9) a claimed solution to the Navier-Stokes existence-and-smoothness problem, one of seven Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize problems ($1M award), produced by an unreleased internal model. OpenAI says the model found a finite-time-blowup configuration (a vortex tightening while energy stays bounded), took 88 hours, used as many as 10,000 concurrent AI agents, and the proof has been formally checked in the Lean proof assistant. The claim is shadowed by a credit dispute: OpenAI says it started the effort 9/1 after hearing a rumor tied to Levent Alpöge (Anthropic) and Tristan Buckmaster (NYU); after finishing its own proof 9/6, OpenAI learned that work actually addressed a related-but-distinct problem (forced Euler equations, not Navier-Stokes). The Clay Mathematics Institute has not yet commented. Read: this is exactly the kind of confirming evidence CLAUDE.md flags for extra skepticism — a lab self-reporting a landmark result on its own unreleased model, formally verified only by the lab itself (Lean-checking confirms internal logical consistency, not that the problem statement was correctly translated or that the result is novel), with no outside mathematician or the prize-granting body yet on record. If it holds up under independent scrutiny, it is a genuine data point for sub-thesis #3 (AI-for-science feedback loops) — a problem open since 1934, solved by brute-force parallel AI reasoning at a scale (10,000 concurrent agents) that would have been compute-inconceivable before this cycle. If it does not hold up, or if the credit dispute reveals OpenAI raced to publish an unverified result to overshadow a rival lab's related work, it undercuts confidence in self-reported AI-for-science claims generally. Logging as Moderate (not Strong) in the Supporting Evidence Tracker pending independent verification, with a new prediction (P036) to track resolution. Sources: OpenAI, Washington Post, Axios (credit controversy), Quanta Magazine.
