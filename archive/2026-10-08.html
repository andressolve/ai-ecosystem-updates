Overnight Developments

bull TSMC Q3 2026: record revenue, beats own guidance top end

TSMC reported Q3 2026 revenue of NT$1.49T (~$46.71B), up 50-51% YoY and above the top of its own $44.6-45.8B guidance range set at the Q2 call. September monthly revenue was NT$511.86B (+54.6% YoY), the second-highest month on record behind August. Analysts estimate net profit (not part of TSMC's own guidance) at roughly +64% YoY to NT$740.8B, though the audited figure and margin/capex detail won't be disclosed until the full Q3 earnings call on 10/15 — TSMC has been in its quiet period since 10/5. This is confirming-magnitude evidence for P010 (TSMC FY26 ≥30% USD growth, already at a 98% ceiling) rather than a new threshold crossing. Reuters · CNBC

bull Samsung preliminary Q3 2026: record ₩107.4T operating profit, beats elevated consensus

Samsung's preliminary Q3 2026 figures: operating profit ₩107.4T (~$80B, up from ₩12.17T a year earlier — roughly 8.8x), beating LSEG SmartEstimate's ₩106.1T consensus by ~1.2% and clearing the ~₩100T whisper number already logged in the 10/6 report. Revenue is estimated at ~₩195T. Management cited surging DRAM/HBM demand from AI data centers as the primary driver — this is Samsung's highest quarterly operating profit ever, by any measure. The stock had sold off into the print (10/6-10/8, on won strength and bonus-cost concerns, not demand softening), despite the beat being broadly telegraphed. Segment detail (HBM vs. conventional DRAM pricing split) is due at the full earnings release later in October. Reuters · CNBC

watch Broadcom, Oracle, and SpaceX simultaneously seeking $100B+ in private-credit AI-chip financing

WSJ and Bloomberg (10/7) reported three separate AI-chip buyers in talks for large private-credit debt deals: Broadcom is working to arrange more than $50B to finance OpenAI's custom AI chip program, with Apollo and Blackstone among the lenders approached (talks early-stage, Broadcom and Oracle reportedly aiming to close before year-end). Oracle is in talks with Apollo and Goldman Sachs to fund a large chip purchase through a separate entity that buys the GPUs and leases them back to Oracle — keeping the debt off Oracle's own balance sheet. SpaceX has discussed a $40B package (~$10B in bank loans plus ~$30B of investment-grade debt, with Pimco among lenders evaluating participation) to fund an Nvidia GPU order, with the GPUs pledged as collateral. Aggregate AI-related borrowing in US leveraged-finance markets is reported to have grown from ~$20B in early 2025 to ~$88B in 2026. This lands on top of the already-logged Broadcom/Anthropic $42-60B facility (10/1-10/2) and Amazon's $8B Nvidia-chip SPV talks (10/2) — a fourth and fifth distinct borrower tapping the same private-credit pool in roughly a week. The GPU-as-collateral structure (SpaceX) is a genuinely new wrinkle: collateral value depends on a rapidly-depreciating, fast-obsoleting asset class, which is the specific fragility scenario the thesis's sub-thesis #5 (financing/value-capture) is designed to flag. None of this bears on whether physical AI demand is real — TSMC's and Samsung's same-week prints argue it is — but it does bear on who bears the risk if any one borrower's revenue doesn't materialize as financed. WSJ via InvestingLive · CNBC (SpaceX) · Bloomberg via TradingKey
