Overnight Developments

watch OpenAI's revenue "correction" was an accounting-convention gap, and the market already round-tripped it

OpenAI disclosed (10/8) its actual annualized revenue is ~$50B — $20B below the ~$68-70B figure that had circulated since a 9/29 Axios/Reuters report. The gap is not lost sales: OpenAI reports net revenue (its own share of partner-channel sales), while the higher figure grossed up partner-channel revenue to make OpenAI comparable to Anthropic's gross-revenue convention. OpenAI reaffirmed 77% total run-rate growth and 107% enterprise run-rate growth for Q3 in the same disclosure — the underlying growth trajectory didn't change.

The market's first reaction was sharp: NVDA -2.94% (-$169B market cap), CoreWeave -7.77%, Oracle -5.48%, Broadcom -4.35%, AMD -3.90%, Super Micro ~-5% on Thursday (10/8). By Friday premarket, the move had largely reversed (see table below) after sell-side analysts called the reaction "overblown." (CNBC, TechCrunch, Qz.com)

Why it matters beyond the round-trip: OpenAI is the single largest disclosed anchor-demand name across NVDA/AVGO/ORCL/CRWV/MSFT exposure simultaneously. A ~3%/$169B single-stock move triggered by a restated accounting convention — not a cancelled order, a capacity cut, or a usage disclosure — shows how little slack the market currently gives any ambiguity in OpenAI's numbers. That's a sentiment-fragility data point, distinct from but adjacent to the balance-sheet financing-fragility axis (Broadcom/Oracle/SpaceX debt-financing talks logged 10/8) this tracker has followed all cycle. Logged Weak-Moderate two-sided; no prediction impact.

bull GlobalFoundries signs $2B deal to make TSMC's CoWoS interposers on US soil

GlobalFoundries agreed to a five-year, $2B deal to produce silicon interposers — the substrate layer connecting AI processors and HBM inside a CoWoS advanced-packaging stack — for TSMC at its Malta, NY fab, the first US-based source for this component. Volume ramp targets 1H 2028. GFS shares rose as much as 7%. (Benzinga, SemiWiki)

Advanced-packaging capacity, not just leading-edge wafers, has been a recurring quantified constraint on AI-chip output this cycle (CoWoS capacity roughly doubling 130K→260K wafer-equivalents/month by 2028, per this tracker's 9/14 entry). A second, US-based CoWoS-ecosystem supplier is a genuine capacity/derisking move — complementary to, not a substitute for, TSMC's own Arizona wafer-fab buildout — though the 2028 timeline means no near-term supply relief.
