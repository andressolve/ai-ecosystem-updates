bear Banks continue shedding AI-data-center loan exposure at a discount

Bloomberg, Reuters and the Financial Times report ING shopped roughly $500M of data-center loans (bids due 11/19, including a $25M slice tied to the Stargate campus in New Mexico plus Vantage- and CyrusOne-linked debt) and Société Générale offered roughly $300M of Oracle-linked debt tied to Wisconsin and Texas projects — together reported as up to $800M by the FT. A separate, single Bloomberg-sourced account (not independently corroborated here) puts HSBC's sale of an Oracle-linked New Mexico loan slice at roughly $300M. Analysts quoted in the FT coverage describe this as shifting risk rather than eliminating it, and the sales reportedly came at steep discounts. This is the clearest concrete financing-fragility data point since the CoreWeave/Oracle CDS widening logged on this thesis in mid-October (CoreWeave's 5-year CDS had moved past 1,000bps, implying a reported ~63% five-year default probability, versus roughly 30% a year earlier) — banks are not waiting for a credit event before de-risking.

bull Dell's Q3 FY2027 beat is the first hard demand read since the governance reversal

Dell's SEC 8-K (after the close, 11/24) reports record quarterly revenue of $54.9B (+103% YoY), a $132.3B AI order backlog (up from $95B entering the quarter) built on $60.2B of new AI orders, and a raised FY2027 revenue guide of $202.0B (+78% YoY) — up sharply from the roughly $140B guide given at the start of the fiscal year. Dell said every order was vetted for customer quality and for access to capital, land and power, and that more than 7,500 customers are now deploying Dell AI Factories. Non-GAAP operating expenses fell to 7% of revenue, which Dell's CFO called the lowest in the company's 42-year history. This print lands the same week equity markets are selling the AI-infrastructure complex on demand-peak and financing-fragility worries — a genuine divergence between what one of the sector's largest integrators is reporting booked and what the market is pricing, worth tracking rather than resolving either way today.

CBS News and a Wall Street Journal opinion piece report that Trump held millions of dollars of NVDA stock while personally pressing NVIDIA's board to reinstate Huang as CEO — a disclosed conflict-of-interest question layered on top of an already-unusual sequence (the board reinstated Huang on 11/23 citing "senior levels of the United States government," six days after Chair Harvey Jones told analysts the board did not intend to reinstate him). Separately, Senate Minority Leader Schumer has demanded "a full accounting" of Trump's defense of Huang, and the House Select Committee on China has reportedly widened its inquiry to seek testimony from both Huang and Trump administration officials — an escalation from the narrower closed-door-briefing request logged previously. Treasury Secretary Bessent's self-set 10-day deadline (from his November 15 Fox News remarks) for the NVIDIA board and outside counsel to answer export-control questions over the Megaspeed matter lands today; no public response from NVIDIA or its outside counsel has been reported as of this writing. None of this affects the SEC civil disclosure probe, the California AG inquiry, or the Senate Banking hearings already scheduled for Huang (12/3) and Lutnick (12/10). A single-sourced HuggingNews item reports that firms linked to "Alice Huang" — the same name already flagged in this thesis's 11/18 entry over a 2022 NVIDIA sales-staff introduction connecting Alibaba to Alice Huang, predating that year's export curbs — pledged $270M in AI servers for China-related loans; this has not been independently verified against a primary source and should be checked before being treated as a confirmed data point.
