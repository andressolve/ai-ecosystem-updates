Overnight Developments

watch Bessent's export-control deadline lapses with no public NVIDIA response

Treasury Secretary Bessent set roughly a ten-day deadline (from ~Nov 15, resolving ~Nov 25) for NVIDIA's board and outside counsel to answer whether the company used shell companies or other structures to evade China export controls, per CBS Face the Nation and Fox News reporting from mid-November. As of this writing, no primary source confirms NVIDIA, its board, or Treasury has addressed the outcome publicly. This sits alongside the SEC's ongoing civil probe into NVIDIA's export-control disclosures (the company stopped reporting Singapore revenue — 22% of the total — the month it blacklisted Megaspeed) and the Dec 3 Senate Banking hearing. Nothing here is new information about underlying facts, but the deadline's silent lapse is itself a status change worth tracking — it leaves an unresolved overhang heading into the Dec 1 closed-door House interview and the Dec 3 public hearing.

bear NVIDIA sharply tightens Southeast Asia export compliance; B300s reselling in China near 2x list price

NVIDIA has cut the number of authorized AI-chip customers across Southeast Asia by more than half — concentrated in Singapore, Malaysia, and Japan — and is now requiring cloud partners on new contracts to keep at least 51% of their business non-Chinese, tied to the Nov 16-17 BIS interim final rule that extended licensing requirements to the region (TechRepublic; DataCenterDynamics). Separately, Reuters reports smuggled B300 servers are reselling inside China around ¥7M (~$1M/unit — roughly 2x the ~$550K US list price), with an active short-term rental market also reported. A HuggingNews item describing "restricted Blackwell chips found at a Shanghai data center linked to Megaspeed" misstates the chip generation: the underlying reporting (Bloomberg, mid-November) concerns H100/H200 servers, not Blackwell. NVIDIA says its own Megaspeed facility inspections (including separate Blackwell warehouses) found no diversion; Megaspeed denies the allegations; US and Singapore authorities are investigating. Read-through: near-term addressable Southeast Asian channel revenue shrinks at the margin as enforcement tightens, while the scale of the China resale premium is itself evidence of unmet compute demand there — a two-sided data point, not a clean signal for the computational-abundance thesis in either direction.

neutral Netherlands formally objects to MATCH Act curbs on ASML

Dutch Trade Minister Sjoerdsma has lobbied Commerce Secretary Lutnick and Congress against the proposed MATCH Act, which would extend US export curbs to ASML's DUV immersion tools (beyond the existing EUV restriction) and give Commerce 150 days to negotiate matching allied controls or impose restrictions on the allies themselves (Ioplus; TheNextWeb). The bill's legislative stage (committee vs. floor) was not confirmed in this pass. This is early-stage policy friction, not yet law — a risk to monitor for ASML's China-exposed DUV business, not a thesis-moving event today.
