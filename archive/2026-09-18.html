Overnight Developments

bull Intel CEO's memory-shortage warning becomes the catalyst for today's Korea rally

Speaking at the AI Infra Summit in Santa Clara on September 15, Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan said memory prices have risen "five to seven times" and that production capacity remains "very limited," adding "many projects are being delayed because they cannot secure enough memory" and that mid/low-end phones and laptops now spend 70-80% of device cost on memory alone. He gave no relief timeline, only that the situation "will get worse." Tan's comments were made three days before today's report but only translated into a clean Korea-session catalyst overnight (Invezz, 9/18): SK Hynix +6.42% and Samsung Electronics +3.37% into the Seoul close, alongside a macro tailwind from easing bond yields. Why this is a higher-quality data point than most of what's already in the tracker: Intel is a memory customer under supply stress, not a supplier with an incentive to talk prices up — this is closer to a "ground truth" read than SK Hynix/Samsung/Micron management commentary, which the framework already discounts as interested-party guidance. Context note added to P015 (DRAM undersupply through C3Q26, held 95%) and P028 (DRAM ASP +50% YoY CY27, held 55%) in predictions/prediction-log.md; logged to theses/computational-abundance.md Supporting Evidence Tracker and to watchlist/tier2/sk-hynix.md / samsung.md.

bull Apple confirms 2027 Samsung memory price hikes — first named-customer contract numbers

DigiTimes (9/16) and MacRumors (9/17) reported Apple has accepted higher Samsung memory pricing for Q1 2027: LPDDR5X moving from ~$1.50/Gb (Q3 2026) to ~$2.00/Gb (+33%), and NAND from ~$0.26/Gb to ~$0.33/Gb (+27%). Notably, Oppo and vivo reportedly refused Samsung's equivalent hikes — a useful reminder that pricing power is uneven across customers and Apple's acceptance reflects its position as a premium/must-supply buyer, not proof the increase clears across the whole market. Still, this is the first hard, named-customer, named-number confirmation this cycle (vs. supplier ASP claims or analyst estimates), and it corroborates Tan's framing from the demand side. No probability change (same reasoning as above — confirms, doesn't newly test, the existing bars).

[bear/watch] CoreWeave upsizes convertible raise to $3.7B; stock falls ~7%

CoreWeave priced an upsized private offering of $3.7B in 2.875% convertible senior notes due 2033 (from an initially announced $3.0B), settling 9/22, and separately established an at-the-market program for up to 35M Class A shares. CRWV fell from an intraday high near $90 to ~$78-80 (-7%) on the combined debt/dilution signal. This is not a new fact pattern — CoreWeave has repeatedly funded backlog conversion with debt/converts over the past two quarters (see watchlist/tier2/coreweave.md) — but it's a fresh, dated instance of the same financing-fragility axis that P008's 9/14-9/17 context notes have been watching since the Fed's 9/16 hike. No hyperscaler or neocloud has yet cited financing cost specifically as a constraint on a call (the P008 trigger), so no probability change, but CoreWeave's willingness to keep raising debt/equity into AI-demand strength is exactly the kind of data point that trigger is designed to catch once (if) financing costs start biting.

neutral Huang reaffirms 2x 2027 chip-sales trajectory at Scotland AI-safety summit

Speaking at King Charles's AI safety gathering at Dumfries House (9/17), Jensen Huang said "I expect Nvidia to sell twice as many chips this next year as we do this year," attributing the growth to nations building localized AI/cloud infrastructure, while separately calling for "good old-fashioned engineering" and AI safety testing. The chip-sales framing is consistent with NVIDIA's 8/26 earnings-call guidance and the Vera Rubin ($40B/GW vs. Blackwell's $25B/GW) revenue trajectory already in the record — not new information — but the venue (a safety-focused royal summit, one day after the 9/17 report flagged it as a low-market-impact event) is notable for pairing bullish supply/demand messaging with safety rhetoric amid the live Amodei "pace the frontier" debate. NVDA traded +2% on 9/17 same-day; today's premarket is roughly flat (-0.11%), consistent with the information already being priced in. No thesis-tracker action.
