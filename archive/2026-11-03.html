Overnight Developments

bear Nasdaq 100 enters a technical correction as AI-financing-fragility concerns broaden and spill into Asia

The Nasdaq 100 fell 1.4% Monday to 27,989.82, officially entering a technical correction (>10% below its October 6 peak); the Nasdaq Composite fell ~1.1%, its worst single-day percentage drop in nearly five months. The proximate catalyst was Oracle pricing a $20B capital raise ($10B in bonds across five tranches, 3-20 years, plus $10B in equity) to fund its AI buildout — notable because bond demand was genuinely strong (~$38B of orders against $10B offered, ~3.8x oversubscribed, per Bloomberg), yet the market read the raise itself as fresh confirmation that even a large, rated AI-capex spender needs external financing to keep pace. Oracle capped further raises at $20B through FY30 while targeting >$225B revenue by FY30. CoreWeave closed Monday at $50.15 (-45% from its October peak over 19 sessions, $35.1B total debt, bond yields above 15%); Nebius fell 8.3%; Applied Digital's CEO said 800MW of its planned data-center capacity still lacks financing. The spillover reached Asia overnight: SK Hynix fell 5.0% and Samsung fell 4.2% at today's Seoul close, with local financial press directly attributing the move to the Oracle-raise-driven AI-financing-strain narrative — notable because both companies posted record Q3 results just two weeks ago (see 10/28-10/29 reports), underscoring that this is a sentiment/financing-model concern, not a fundamentals reversal. Distinguish from the 11/2 CoreWeave/Nebius/Nscale "shut out of the bond market" story: Oracle's bonds clearing 3.8x oversubscribed shows credit access for large issuers remains open — the market is pricing concern about the AI-capex funding model broadly, not a fresh access constraint. (Bloomberg, 24/7 Wall St., Seoul Economic Daily)

bear Kioxia's NAND deceleration warning cracks the memory-supercycle narrative — but it's NAND, not DRAM

Kioxia guided December-quarter operating profit to ¥2.0T, missing the ¥2.35T Street estimate and showing only slight growth from September's ¥1.99T. NAND average selling prices rose "only" mid-20s% QoQ in the September quarter, down sharply from +70% QoQ the prior quarter. Kioxia shares fell as much as 17% Monday (worst day in three months); Western Digital fell 5%, SanDisk 3%, and Micron 4% in sympathy (Micron has since bounced, +3.74% in today's premarket). This is the first hard evidence from a major memory supplier that AI-driven demand growth is decelerating in at least one product category — but Kioxia is a NAND-flash pure-play, and P015 (DRAM undersupply through C3Q26, 95%) is specifically about DRAM contract pricing. SK Hynix and Samsung are both DRAM/HBM-weighted, not NAND-primary, so the direct read-through to their earnings is limited; their declines today are better explained by the broader Nasdaq-correction spillover above. Logged as adjacent disconfirming evidence, explicitly caveated. (Bloomberg, Japan Times)

watch NVIDIA reportedly orders data-center partners to cap China exposure on new contracts

The Financial Times reports NVIDIA has told cloud/data-center partners that new contracts must allocate at least 51% of capacity to non-Chinese customers — delivered verbally by sales and regional teams, with no written policy. NVIDIA neither confirmed nor denied the report, saying only that "datacenters and clouds built on American chips are the foundation that American model and application makers need to compete worldwide." Only new contracts are affected; existing agreements are untouched. This reads as NVIDIA self-imposing a constraint on the offshore-cloud-rental channel that has been a partial workaround for direct China chip-export restrictions — likely preempting tighter government enforcement rather than responding to a new mandate. NVIDIA's own China DC revenue is already modeled at $0 (per its Q1 FY27 call), so the direct financial impact is unclear; watch for confirmation or quantification on the next earnings call. (Bloomberg, Reuters via Investing.com)

neutral Palantir beats on revenue and raises guidance, but falls ~7-8% premarket on bookings deceleration

Q3 2026 revenue was $2.31B (+96% YoY, first quarter above $2B), beating the ~$2.18B consensus; adjusted EPS $0.44 vs. $0.41 consensus. US Commercial revenue reached $984M (+148% YoY), surpassing US government revenue for the first time in company history. FY26 guidance was raised to $8.530-8.534B. Despite the beat, the stock fell as much as 8% premarket because US commercial bookings (total contract value) grew only +11% YoY, a sharp deceleration from +153% YoY last quarter — at Palantir's ~165x earnings multiple, the market is pricing forward bookings growth, not trailing revenue, exactly the risk flagged in yesterday's report. This is the cleanest public proxy we track for enterprise AI adoption (application layer); one quarter of bookings deceleration could be deal-timing noise or a real inflection — worth confirming against Q4. (Palantir Q3 2026 earnings release, SEC 8-K)
