bull Broadcom Q3 FY2026: AI revenue beats, guide clears the FY27 bar early — but the stock fell anyway

Broadcom reported Q3 FY2026 results after Tuesday's close: consolidated revenue $29.6B (+86% YoY), AI semiconductor revenue $16.7B (+221% YoY, +54% QoQ), beating the company's own $16.0B guide. Q4 FY26 guide: AI revenue $21.7B (+236% YoY), consolidated revenue $34.8B (+93% YoY). Annualizing Q4's AI guide alone produces an ~$86.8B run-rate — already well above the $60B FY27 bar this tracker set in March (P020), before fiscal 2027 has even begun. FY26 full-year AI revenue is now guided to ~$56B (+180% YoY); management reaffirmed (not raised) the FY2027 AI target of >$100B for the second consecutive quarter. The AI XPU Platform (the $35B+ Apollo/Blackstone financing vehicle launched 6/3) was confirmed on the call as financing mainly Anthropic and OpenAI capacity, with other customers self-funding. Despite the AI-specific beat-and-raise, AVGO fell >3% in after-hours and premarket trading — the Q4 total-revenue guide landed a hair under the ~$35.03B Street consensus, and consolidated gross margin compressed 210bps QoQ to 75% (Semiconductor Solutions segment ~67%) on AI-mix dilution. This is the same "world vs market" pattern that drove the 6/3 post-print selloff: the AI-demand trajectory keeps confirming, while the stock prices margin quality and the gap between guidance and buyside whisper numbers. neutral NVIDIA confirms $12.93B Hugging Face acquisition

NVIDIA's official blog (primary source, 9/3) confirmed the company has agreed to acquire Hugging Face for $12.93B, with Jensen Huang framing NVIDIA's role as strengthening infrastructure, reliability, safety, and deployment while Hugging Face retains its brand and independence. This closes out three weeks of unconfirmed reporting: 8/27 (~$12.9-13B talks), 9/2 (~$14B, "could close this week"). Hugging Face hosts 3M+ models and 500K+ datasets for 18M+ developers across 200K+ companies, against ~$150M of disclosed annual revenue — an ~86x revenue multiple justified only by strategic platform value. This is NVIDIA's second-largest acquisition after the ~$20B Groq deal (Dec 2025), and notably is structured as a conventional acquisition rather than the license+acqui-hire pattern NVIDIA has used for Groq/Poolside/Rebellions to reduce HSR exposure — worth watching for regulatory commentary given the platform-concentration optics (NVIDIA now owns compute, and increasingly the open-model distribution layer on top of it). No prediction impact; logged two-sided to the computational abundance thesis (sub-thesis #4).
