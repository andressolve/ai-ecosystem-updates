Overnight Developments

bear S&P Global cuts Meta's credit outlook to negative on AI capex/cash-flow risk

S&P Global Ratings revised Meta's outlook from stable to negative, citing the $135-145B 2026 capex plan and projecting free operating cash flow deficits of -$39B in 2026 and -$71B in 2027, with FOCF potentially staying negative beyond 2028. S&P said Meta's "ability to monetize its AI investments is more limited than that of its peers" — a differentiated, falsifiable knock on Meta specifically rather than a sector-wide call. This is not a downgrade; S&P is flagging trajectory, not acting on the rating today. It is the first rating-agency action this cycle on a Big-4 hyperscaler, extending the pattern already logged from Meta's first negative-FCF quarter (10/22) and its $95B equity raise (10/26) to a formal external credit opinion. (GuruFocus, Fortune)

bear CoreWeave, Nebius, and Nscale "effectively shut out" of the bond market

WSJ reports the three neoclouds have moved from merely paying more for credit to struggling to access it at all in recent weeks, as lenders and investors demand tougher terms. CoreWeave bonds issued earlier this year at a 9.75% coupon now trade at yields above 15%; its 5yr CDS has climbed above 1,100bps — elevated, but inside the ~1,190bps record already logged on 10/19. What's available is secured, contract-backed capital — the type of structure behind Nebius's existing $3.5B Citi facility, consistent with CEO Arkady Volozh's own 10/26 comment that customer-advance financing, not open debt markets, is Nebius's path. Pimco CIO Dan Ivascyn: "the bar is going higher and higher in terms of proving to lenders that this investment will turn into sufficient revenue." Still no actual default, covenant breach, or failed refinancing — the concrete escalation this thread has been watching for since late October. (WSJ via aiweekly.co, Dealroom)

neutral Marvell to acquire Lumentum for $115B, building an AI-optics leader

Marvell agreed to acquire data-center optics maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal valued at $115B enterprise value — Marvell shareholders get 67% of the combined company, paying $1,132/share (a 23.9% premium to Lumentum's October 28 unaffected close). The combination targets what the companies claim is a ~$550B total addressable market in AI connectivity as networks move beyond copper to optical interconnects, with over $1B in claimed annual run-rate cost synergies. NVIDIA invested ~$2B in each company earlier this year, making it a strategic shareholder on both sides of the deal. Marvell is Broadcom's most direct comp in custom silicon and attach chips; this concentrates Marvell's bet specifically on the optical layer that is also the largest physical constraint on Broadcom's own Spectrum-X switch roadmap. (Bloomberg, Reuters via Investing.com)

watch NVIDIA's Vera Rubin VR200 rack ramp slips 1-2 months on PCB defects

Reports out of Taiwan's supply chain indicate Vera Rubin VR200 rack-cabinet volume production is delayed roughly one to two months into Q4 2026, caused by defects in midplane PCBs and passive components — a named board partner encountered an impedance mismatch during validation that degraded high-speed signal integrity. This reads as a supply-chain quality issue, not a design or demand problem, but it's a concrete execution data point worth tracking into NVIDIA's next earnings commentary. (Reported via Taiwan supply-chain trade press, 11/2 — not yet addressed by NVIDIA directly)
