Overnight Developments

bull Samsung/SK Hynix memory inventories fall below 10 days; 2027 DRAM/HBM capacity effectively sold out

KB Securities (Korean brokerage, 9/7 report) says Samsung and SK Hynix memory inventories have fallen below 10 days of supply, as simultaneous AI-driven demand for HBM, server DRAM, and enterprise SSDs collides with capacity already committed elsewhere. KB warns 2027 could be "the worst shortage in history." Corroborating trade reporting (Digitimes, Korea JoongAng Daily, BusinessKorea) states Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron's 2027 DRAM/HBM capacity is now effectively sold out with no additional supply available, with demand projected to exceed supply by 10+ percentage points next year. This is a sharper, more physical confirmation than prior evidence in this thread (which has mostly been ASP/guidance-based) — sub-10-days inventory is a stockout metric, not a pricing signal, and it directly corroborates the 8/26-logged mechanism by which NVIDIA's own gross-margin guide is compressing on rising HBM/memory input costs. Kospi chip complex responded hard: SK Hynix +8.26% to 1,783,000 KRW, Samsung +5.68% to 270,000 KRW (Seoul close, 9/7), extending Friday's Broadcom-linked two-day move; TSMC also +2.07% (Taiwan close). Caveat: this is Korean-brokerage commentary, not a primary Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron IR disclosure — treat the specific "<10 days" and "worst shortage in history" framing as an estimate pending primary confirmation (e.g., a 10-Q inventory-turns disclosure). No probability change to P015 (95%) or P028 (55%) — both are already near-ceiling; this is magnitude confirmation of an already-high-confidence view, not a new threshold crossing. OpenAI itself has not made this claim — President Greg Brockman's own 9/3 launch framing ("welcome to the AGI era") stopped short of declaring AGI achieved. This escalates but doesn't newly resolve the self-gating/hype-framing thread opened with the Astra launch: the party with the largest direct financial interest in the continued AI-capex narrative (NVIDIA's own FY28 70% growth guide, 8/26, is a supply-constrained bet on exactly this kind of demand continuing) is now making a stronger capability claim than the model's own developer will make on the record. Treat as a rhetoric/sentiment data point, not a capability confirmation, until independent benchmark replication — still outstanding since 9/3 — exists. No prediction impact. bear Micron's first Taiwan union mediation ends without a deal

Micron held its first labor mediation session with its Taoyuan (Taiwan) union on 9/4; it ended without an agreement, the strike alert has not been lifted, and a second mediation session is scheduled. The two Taiwan unions (~10,000 combined members, ~80% strike-support in an August survey) are seeking a one-off FY26 bonus sized at roughly 83 months' salary per employee, plus a switch to quarterly bonuses set at 15% of operating profit starting FY27. Micron says its 2026 compensation package will be the highest in company history but has not met the union's specific asks. This is a real, quantifiable operational risk landing directly on top of today's shortage headline: Micron's own regulatory filings state a majority of its 2025 DRAM output came from Taiwan, so a prolonged strike would constrain output at exactly the moment brokerage reporting says industry capacity is already sold out through 2027. Two-sided in practice — bearish for Micron's own shipment volume and customer relationships, but a Micron-specific supply disruption would arguably tighten (not loosen) industry-wide DRAM pricing power. No impact to P015/P028/P029. Logged Weak-Moderate disconfirming in theses/computational-abundance.md and watchlist/tier2/micron.md.
