Overnight Developments

bear BIS closes China's AI-chip remote-access loophole, rescinds the AI Diffusion Rule

The Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule, "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors" (RIN 0694-AK50), effective on publication (11/16). Data centers in third countries serving China- or Macau-headquartered entities above roughly 12 million aggregate TPP (reported as roughly 760 H100-class or 200 B300-class GPUs, with some outlets giving slightly different equivalences) now need a US export license; existing operators get a 90-day window to apply. New case-by-case licensing is extended to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, and Brazil as "higher-than-normal diversion risk" jurisdictions. The rule formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule. Multiple trade-compliance outlets (a law-firm client alert, two trade-press sites) report the rule's own text states it was "written by and at the direction of the Department of Treasury and the National Security Council" — Commerce normally authors its own export rules — and that Politico has reported Treasury Secretary Bessent forced the rule through Commerce Secretary Lutnick's department after a months-long internal dispute connected to the Megaspeed/Jensen Huang espionage allegations. Same-day reaction: Baidu fell to a record low (-5.8%), Alibaba -5%, Hang Seng Tech -1.1%, while domestic Chinese substitutes rallied (SMIC +6.8%, Hua Hong +8%). This is the most concrete, dollar-and-threshold-quantified US-China AI chip trade action logged on this thesis's political-risk axis — distinct from the domestic data-center-moratorium axis tracked since early November, which concerns permitting, not export policy. It is two-sided for the core compute-demand thesis: it closes a channel that was inflating China-linked access to NVIDIA-class compute (narrowly disconfirming for "unconstrained global demand"), but it is close to irrelevant to the US hyperscaler buildout that drives the overwhelming majority of the demand this thesis tracks. It is, however, a new and NVDA-specific governance complication landing hours before tonight's earnings call. (Nixon Peabody; Converge Digest; TheNextWeb; mlq.ai)

watch NVIDIA's reported response — a board export-control committee and a China-leasing sales bar — is unverified

Reporting relayed through an AI news aggregator (citing CNBC sources and NVIDIA's own X account, neither independently fetched this session) claims NVIDIA created a new board-level "National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee" (reportedly chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson, whose board seat and Audit Committee membership are independently confirmed via NVIDIA's own site) and that NVIDIA will bar chip sales to any buyer leasing more than 50% of its GPUs to Chinese firms. The reported trigger is a 10-day ultimatum from Bessent, separately corroborated by Reuters-sourced reporting (11/15) that he demanded the board explain the Megaspeed matter and separately pushed for the next CEO to be hired from outside the company. I could not confirm the committee or the sales-bar policy against nvidianews.nvidia.com, investor.nvidia.com, or an 8-K in this session — flagging per standing instructions on sourcing gaps rather than asserting either as fact.
