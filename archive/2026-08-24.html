Overnight Developments

watch NVIDIA pays Poolside $6B for a model license, invests $1B; separately in early talks to back Perplexity

Newcomer first reported (8/20), corroborated by Bloomberg same-day and independently confirmed by WSJ (8/23): NVIDIA is paying AI coding-model startup Poolside a $6B non-exclusive license fee for its "Model Factory" (the system behind Poolside's open-weight Laguna models), plus a separate $1B direct equity investment at a $12B pre-money valuation (up from Poolside's prior ~$3B mark). 109 Poolside employees transfer to NVIDIA's Nemotron project; Poolside's three co-founders remain to run the independent shell entity — the same license-plus-acqui-hire structure as the December 2025 Groq deal (~$20B), now a three-time pattern. WSJ frames the strategic rationale as building a powerful US open-weight model to compete with Chinese open-weight leaders (DeepSeek, Kimi K3). Separately, The Information (8/23, unconfirmed) reports NVIDIA is in early talks to invest in Perplexity AI at a $30B+ valuation (up from ~$23B in January 2026) — a conventional strategic investment, not a license+acqui-hire structure. Both deals extend NVIDIA's downstream-in-the-stack strategy: investing in and licensing IP from application-layer companies, not just selling them chips, while the license+acqui-hire structure continues to sidestep HSR/antitrust scrutiny (same read as Groq). No prediction impact. Logged to theses/computational-abundance.md and watchlist/tier1/nvda.md.

watch Anthropic hires Google's founding TPU architect, signaling a possible in-house chip program

Bloomberg (Dina Bass, 8/21), corroborated by multiple secondary pickups: Anthropic hired Amir Salek — founder and former head of Google's TPU program, who led development of the first seven TPU generations (2013-2022) — reporting to James Bradbury, Anthropic's head of compute/inference infrastructure. Reporting explicitly frames the hire as Anthropic "laying groundwork for its own custom silicon," a proprietary chip program distinct from and additive to its existing Google TPU and Amazon Trainium supply relationships. No Anthropic on-record confirmation, no product roadmap, no chip specs or timeline disclosed — this is a hire, not a program launch, and should be treated as a leading indicator only. If it matures into an actual in-house ASIC (following the OpenAI/Broadcom Jalapeño and Google TPU playbooks), Anthropic would become the fourth major lab with a proprietary silicon program, a new Anthropic-specific data point for sub-thesis #4's custom-silicon-proliferation claim. Open question: would an in-house Anthropic chip compete with or complement its current TPU/Trainium allocation, and does it bear on the durability of the reported Broadcom-Anthropic debt-financing structure (8/20-21 entry)? No prediction change — P003 (custom-silicon-majority-by-2026) is not tested by a hire with a multi-year build timeline. Logged to theses/computational-abundance.md.
