Overnight Developments

watch SK Hynix in talks with Intel on first-ever US-made memory chips

Reuters (exclusive, 9/15 22:32 PT) reported SK Hynix is in early, exploratory talks with Intel about producing memory chips on US soil — a first for the company, which currently has no US wafer fab (its only US footprint is the Indiana advanced-packaging/R&D site, cleanroom operations targeted H2 2028). Reported deal structures include SK Hynix leasing part of Intel's long-delayed Ohio semiconductor complex, or a joint venture including Intel and AI hyperscalers seeking supply security. No decision on structure or chip type. SK Hynix's own statement (news.skhynix.com, 9/16): "No plans have been confirmed regarding the reported talks." Intel +4.85% premarket to ~$101.85 (MarketScreener); SK Hynix's Seoul session closed +4.08% — the Reuters story broke roughly an hour before the 15:30 KST close, a plausible (evidence-based, not guessed) contributor to the late-session strength. Reuters separately flags South Korean government opposition as a real risk, since advanced memory technology is treated as a national-security asset domestically. Why this matters beyond the stock pop: it's the first reported pathway to geographically diversifying advanced DRAM/HBM production outside Korea/Taiwan-adjacent supply chains — relevant to the "who captures value" and geopolitical-concentration questions this framework tracks, distinct from the near-term supply-tightness thesis (P015/P028/P029), which this doesn't move. Logged in watchlist/tier2/sk-hynix.md.

watch OpenAI fields investor interest in $1.2-1.5T valuation round; no 2026 IPO

Bloomberg/FT/WSJ (9/15 evening) reported OpenAI is in early talks with investors about a new funding round — investors initially approached at a $1.2T valuation, with OpenAI reportedly pushing for $1.5T, versus the $852B mark set by March's $122B round. Talks are early-stage and investor-initiated; the figure could move. Separately, Altman has ruled out a 2026 IPO ("an ill-advised moment to go public"), with OpenAI's CFO telling staff the company expects to go public in 2027. A $1.5T ask would be roughly on par with NVIDIA-scale public-market valuations for a company that reported ~$20B ARR as of late 2025 — a valuation-vs-revenue gap worth watching if the round firms up. No thesis-tracker impact today (informational; logged to watchlist/tier3/openai.md).

neutral CoreWeave brings up first multi-rack Vera Rubin NVL72 cluster

CoreWeave (company release, 9/16) announced it connected multiple NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 racks (72 Rubin GPUs + 36 Vera CPUs each, NVLink 6, Spectrum-X Ethernet) into a single multi-rack, scale-out cluster spanning hundreds of GPUs — the first AI cloud provider to do so, following its June 2026 single-rack bring-up/validation milestone. A genuine operational proof point that Rubin is progressing from single-rack validation to scaled deployment on schedule; modestly supportive of the NVDA Rubin-ramp trajectory (P017-adjacent) but not itself a new demand or revenue datapoint.
