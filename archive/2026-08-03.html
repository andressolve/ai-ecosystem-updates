Overnight Developments

bear CoreWeave's term loan syndication is the cycle's first neocloud loan-market stress signal

CoreWeave's $2.6B first-lien delayed-draw term loan (JPMorgan-led, due 2031) launched at S+425-450/99 OID and had to be flexed to S+550/96-97 OID (~10.44% YTM) to clear — a ~100-125bp widening — with lenders extracting a 1.35x DSCR maintenance covenant, full amortization, a parent-revolver-covenant tie-in until CoreWeave is investment-grade rated, and a $112.5M minimum-liquidity requirement (Tech Times 8/3, PitchBook LCD). This lands alongside CoreWeave's 5-year CDS hitting ~855bps on 7/29 (~50% implied 5-year default probability) and Q1 2026 interest expense of $536M (46% of adjusted EBITDA) against -$4.71B FCF. Every prior sub-thesis #5 datapoint this cycle (AVGO-Apollo-Blackstone, NVDA-DSX, NVDA's own 5yr CDS spike, Meta's SPVs) was either an equity-market reaction or a reported deal structure; this is professional credit investors actually repricing a live syndication and winning real covenant protection — a harder data type. The deal still cleared (not pulled, priced near-par at 96-97, not distressed), which argues credit markets remain willing to fund the buildout, just at a materially higher price with real protection attached — the early stage of a financing-fragility cycle rather than a closure of access. CoreWeave is also the most levered name in the neocloud tier by design, so this is not yet evidence of a broader credit event. Watch: a second neocloud forced into similar repricing, CDS breaching 1000bps, or a DSCR miss on the delayed-draw facility.
