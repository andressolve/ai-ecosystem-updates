Overnight Developments

watch ASML/TSMC 12-inch photomask initiative; Samsung expands ASML partnership to DRAM

ASML and TSMC announced (9/7, ahead of the SPIE Bacus Conference) a collaborative industry initiative to transition to 12-inch (large-format) photomasks to maximize High NA EUV output, targeting a 12-inch mask pilot line by 2031 and full lithography-system readiness for advanced-node production by 2033. TSMC confirmed it intends to use High NA EUV in high-volume manufacturing starting 2030, with the number of layers requiring High NA rising as transistor architectures grow more complex for AI applications. Separately (9/8), Samsung Electronics and ASML announced an expansion of their long-standing strategic partnership: Samsung joins the same 12-inch photomask initiative and plans to introduce High NA EUV into DRAM high-volume manufacturing by 2028. Primary sources: ASML press release, TSMC press release, Samsung Global Newsroom. ASML +4.20% (Amsterdam, live 9/8 session) — the clearest explanation available for today's move. Read: this is a multi-year capital-commitment confirmation, not a bookings or revenue datapoint — three of the industry's most capitally disciplined firms jointly committing through 2033 only makes sense if all three still expect AI-driven leading-edge demand to be growing at the end of the decade. Samsung's DRAM-first timeline is a supply-side signal worth reading alongside the 9/7 KB Securities inventory-shortage report, not against it. Logged Moderate supporting in theses/computational-abundance.md.

bull Firmus/OpenAI Malaysia deal and Palantir/Nebius sovereign AI partnership

Nvidia-backed Firmus (last valued >$10.5B; backed by Nvidia, Jane Street, Blackstone funds, Coatue) signed a multi-year deal making OpenAI an anchor customer across two Malaysian data centers, lifting Firmus's total contracted capacity across customers above 900MW ahead of a rumored IPO (Reuters, 9/8). Separately, Palantir named Nebius its preferred sovereign AI infrastructure partner: following an integration period, eligible Palantir commercial customers will access Nebius compute/inference infrastructure from inside the Palantir enterprise perimeter, including hosting and adapting open-weight models with customer-owned data; the two companies also committed to joint modular data-center deployments at sites where power is already available (Business Wire, 9/8). NBIS +2.69% premarket. Read: both are genuine, incremental compute-demand confirmations distinct from hyperscaler capex guides — Firmus/OpenAI extends OpenAI's non-hyperscaler compute-sourcing pattern (Lambda, Nscale, now Firmus) into Southeast Asia, while Palantir/Nebius converts Nebius from a pure GPU-cloud reseller into an embedded infrastructure layer inside a major enterprise-software distribution channel. Neither is large enough alone to move a thesis probability. Logged Moderate supporting in theses/computational-abundance.md.

neutral Anthropic walks away from $6B Decart deal; IPO-ratings push; UK Aria chair resigns over conflict of interest

Anthropic walked away from a reported ~$6B acquisition of Decart (an AI-efficiency/inference-optimization startup) after performing due diligence — it would have been Anthropic's largest acquisition to date, scrapped weeks before its planned fall IPO (Bloomberg, 9/8). Separately, the Financial Times (9/7-9/8) reports bankers advising both Anthropic and OpenAI on their prospective IPOs are pushing both companies toward investment-grade credit ratings post-listing, to lower AI-infrastructure borrowing costs. Also separately, Matt Clifford — chair of the UK's Aria research agency and an architect of UK AI policy — is stepping down (effective November 6) after taking a full-time Anthropic role leading its non-US government engagement, following a formal conflict-of-interest complaint from the Labour chair of the Commons science committee (The Guardian, 9/7-9/8). Read: the Decart walk-away cuts two ways — pre-IPO capital discipline (bull) versus a deal that didn't clear its own diligence bar (bear); no on-record reason from either side. The investment-grade-ratings push continues the financing-fragility thread this tracker has followed since early August (30yr Treasury yields, AI-debt issuance attribution) — it only matters as a strategy if the market is currently pricing AI-lab credit at a premium to investment grade. The Clifford resignation is a governance/reputational friction point specific to Anthropic, not a demand or compute-cost signal. Logged Weak-Moderate two-sided (informal sub-thesis #5) in theses/computational-abundance.md.
