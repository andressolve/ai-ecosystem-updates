Overnight Developments

watch NVIDIA's 8-K puts a $105B hard cap on the SB Energy/OpenAI Ohio guarantee

NVIDIA's Form 8-K (Item 1.01/2.03, filed 8/17, signed by CFO Colette Kress) discloses the mechanics behind Sunday's SB Energy/PORTS-Pike announcement: NVIDIA entered "multiple residual value guaranties" tied to leases on the initial 4.25 IT-GW phase of the Ohio campus, with NVIDIA's aggregate payment obligation "cumulatively capped at $105 billion." The guarantee pays out only if the tenant — an OpenAI Group PBC affiliate, signed to a 20-year anchor lease — defaults or becomes insolvent, and OpenAI has contractually agreed to indemnify/reimburse NVIDIA for any amount actually paid out. The remaining ~3.75-3.8 IT-GW option on the 8 IT-GW total campus is not covered by the $105B cap; further credit support there is "exercisable in NVIDIA's sole discretion," meaning aggregate exposure could still grow.

This is the first time NVIDIA has put a primary, SEC-filed number on its own aggregate guarantee exposure, superseding both the WSJ's unconfirmed "<$120B" estimate (8/16) and the earlier unconfirmed $250B figure (7/27). Two-sided read: a 20-year OpenAI-anchored lease at this scale is the clearest single demand-durability signal yet in the vendor-financing arc tracked since 8/10, but NVIDIA now carries a concrete, single-customer-concentrated $105B contingent liability — on a per-GW basis (~$24.7B/GW on the 4.25GW phase) this is actually higher than BofA's modeled Broadcom exposure (~$18.5B/GW at full 20GW scale), though the two structures aren't directly comparable (RVG cap vs. platform-wide modeled max loss — treat the comparison as a back-of-envelope guess, not apples-to-apples). Economically, this is a bet on OpenAI's solvency over a 20-year horizon. No change to P003 (compute-mix prediction; this is financing-structure information). Logged to theses/computational-abundance.md and watchlist/tier1/nvda.md.

neutral Nebius Vineland: zoning approval ≠ stop-work resolution

Local coverage (NBC10 Philadelphia, 8/17-18) reports the Vineland Planning Board approved a "Phase 2" zoning expansion (600,000 sq ft addition, targeting 2.6M sq ft total campus) for the site anchoring Nebius's $17.4B Microsoft deal. Separately, WHYY (primary, quoting the City of Vineland directly) confirms the stop-work orders first reported 8/13-14 were real: two stop-construction orders (Aug 6 on an LNG storage tank; Aug 10 on a Bloom Energy fuel-cell install), both for unpermitted work, carrying $2,000/day fine exposure — and these remain open. The zoning vote and the permit violations are two distinct regulatory tracks; the former does not resolve the latter. Nebius's CCO reiterated "on track" only via secondary reporting, with no company statement addressing the stop-work orders specifically. NBIS's premarket reaction was muted/mixed, not a clean "hurdle cleared" repricing — the market isn't treating this as resolved either. Logged to watchlist/tier2/nebius.md.
