Overnight Developments

bull Google-Constellation: 3.6 GW, 20-year nuclear + PJM power deal

Google and Constellation Energy announced a 20-year power purchase structure covering 3,590 MW in the PJM grid: 890 MW from uprating 11 existing nuclear reactors across Illinois, Pennsylvania, and New Jersey (first upgraded unit expected online 2028), plus a separate long-term agreement for 2,700 MW of general PJM supply. The deal enables more than $4.3B of new Constellation investment. This is the clearest instance yet of a hyperscaler paying to expand physical generation capacity rather than simply contracting for power that already exists — nuclear uprates are also one of the few capacity additions that can plausibly land inside a 2028 timeline, versus a decade-plus for new-build reactors. Caveat: first new electrons don't arrive for roughly two years, so this adds to the medium-term power pipeline without relieving 2026-2027 constraints. CNBC · Reuters via Sowetan

watch Microsoft cuts internal Claude spend >1/3; Meta's internal Claude Code users roughly halve

The Information reports Microsoft slashed its projected 2026 internal spending on Anthropic's Claude by more than a third (from an initial estimate of at least $1B), nudging developers toward GitHub Copilot by default; Meta's internal Claude Code user count fell from roughly 60,000 to 30,000 as the company promotes in-house Muse Code/MetaCode. Both companies frame this as a control/cost-standardization move for internal tooling. Importantly, the large capital relationships are explicitly unaffected: Microsoft's up-to-$5B equity investment in Anthropic continues, Anthropic's $30B Azure compute-purchase commitment continues, and third-party customer spend on Claude via Azure and Bedrock is reported still growing. The split is informative on its own terms — the compute-demand side of these relationships (what this framework tracks) is intact while the softer distribution-stickiness layer erodes at two of Anthropic's largest corporate partners simultaneously, a data point on how much pricing power frontier labs retain once a hyperscaler builds a credible in-house coding assistant. watch CXMT poaches 27 ex-Samsung/SK Hynix engineers; both stocks sell off ahead of Q3

A Korean National Assembly office (Rep. Choi Soo-jin) disclosed that 27 engineers who worked an average of 15.4 years at Samsung Electronics or SK Hynix — 416 combined years, including 6 chief/executive-level staff — moved to China's CXMT, recruited with 60-100% pay premiums plus job-security and relocation incentives. Average CXMT tenure was only 2.9 years, and 26 of the 27 have already left the company, which tempers a clean "brain drain" narrative even as it adds a concrete talent/IP-leakage channel to the 9/28 CXMT market-share entry already in the thesis tracker. Separately and on the same morning (not causally linked per reporting), Samsung and SK Hynix shares both fell in Seoul ahead of Q3 earnings — Samsung is expected to post a record ~100 trillion won ($74B) quarterly operating profit when it reports preliminary results Thursday 10/8, with SK Hynix reporting later in October — with won strength and higher annual-bonus cost expectations cited as the drag, alongside lingering sentiment pressure from Amazon's 10/5 $68B blocked-data-center disclosure.
