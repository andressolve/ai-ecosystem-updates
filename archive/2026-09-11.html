Overnight Developments

bear DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash claims a smaller HBM/SSD footprint — first named-model evidence for the memory-efficiency disconfirming path

DeepSeek's new V4.1 Flash model uses a more efficient architecture and a reduced KV cache, which the company says lowers the HBM and SSD volume required to run it (Bloomberg, 9/11; Seeking Alpha, 9/10-9/11). Samsung Electronics fell -3.53% and SK Hynix -2.21% in the Seoul session on the news; Micron and SanDisk were reported to hold up better, consistent with a DRAM/HBM-specific (not broad-memory) read. This is the first time this cycle that a named, released model — not a hypothetical scaling-law argument — has been the proximate cause of a memory-stock selloff on efficiency grounds. It directly targets the disconfirming-evidence category CLAUDE.md flags for the computational abundance thesis ("efficiency gains outpacing demand") and the demand-side assumption underlying P015 (DRAM undersupply persists through C3Q26, currently 95%). Important caveats: (1) DeepSeek's efficiency claims have a history of not surviving independent verification at face value (the Jan 2025 R1 episode); (2) the mechanism described — leaner architecture + smaller KV cache — reduces memory per model/per token, which is the same kind of efficiency gain that historically has been outrun by usage growth (Jevons paradox pattern this thesis has bet on); (3) P015 resolves on C3Q26 contract pricing data due ~Oct 2026 — a near-term data point this development doesn't have time to move before resolution. No probability change. Logged to the thesis Disconfirming Evidence Tracker and to P015's notes as a monitoring item, not a repricing.

bull Oracle posts a clean Q1 FY27 beat-and-raise; likely catalyst for this morning's broad AI-infra rally

Oracle (AMC 9/10): revenue $19.35B (+30% YoY), non-GAAP EPS $1.92, both above estimates. Cloud infrastructure (IaaS) revenue +121% YoY to $7.4B; combined cloud (IaaS+SaaS) +62% to $11.6B. watch Microsoft reportedly plans to triple data-center capacity to ~38GW by 2032

Bloomberg reported (9/10, corroborated by Reuters/Seeking Alpha/Investing.com same day) that Microsoft's internal roadmap targets ~38GW of total data-center power capacity by 2032, up from ~12GW today — a footprint Bloomberg says would exceed New York state's peak electricity consumption. About a third of the 2032 total would be AI-chip capacity, up from roughly 2GW of the current 12GW. The plan spans owned and leased facilities and is attributed to demand strong enough that Microsoft has turned away some business. This is sourced to Bloomberg's reporting on an internal plan, not an MSFT public confirmation or filing — label accordingly. If accurate, it is one of the largest single capex signals logged this cycle and squarely supports the computational abundance thesis's core claim that current compute-demand estimates are structurally too low. Logged to the Supporting Evidence Tracker as Moderate strength pending MSFT's own confirmation (next likely at Q1 FY27 earnings, date TBD).
