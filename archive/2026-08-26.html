Overnight Developments

watch OpenAI's Jalapeño chip beats NVIDIA's Blackwell in first independent inference benchmarks, night before NVDA earnings

OpenAI and Broadcom presented Jalapeño — OpenAI's first custom accelerator, co-developed with Broadcom and unveiled as a tape-out 6/24 — at Hot Chips 2026 (8/25), alongside the first published third-party benchmarks. SemiAnalysis, given lab access to run its InferenceX suite, reported the 700W part delivering 1.5-1.9x more throughput per kilowatt and 1.7-3.6x lower end-to-end latency than NVIDIA's GB200/GB300 (Blackwell) rack systems across three open models (GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek R1 670B, Kimi K2.5 1T). OpenAI's own blog post (primary source) confirms Jalapeño is "the first step in a multi-generation compute platform," targeting initial deployment "in very small volumes" by end of 2026, more meaningful volume in 2027. Caveats: this is inference only — NVIDIA's training workload dominance is untested and unaddressed; the comparison is against NVIDIA's current-generation Blackwell, not the upcoming Vera Rubin; and OpenAI/SemiAnalysis chose the benchmark suite and models. Read-through: bullish confirmation for AVGO's custom-silicon franchise (a third-party-verified, competitive product from its largest disclosed XPU customer) — logged to theses/computational-abundance.md (sub-thesis #4, Strong) and watchlist/tier1/avgo.md. Bearish-tail context for NVIDIA specifically on inference share, published hours before tonight's print — worth watching for any CFO/CEO commentary addressing custom-silicon competition on the call. No prediction changes; P024 stays at 95% (the resolution gate is a funding/order announcement, not a performance claim). Sources: OpenAI, SemiAnalysis, TechCrunch, Tom's Hardware.

neutral Evercore frames Meta as a "merchant seller" of excess AI compute — $22B/yr potential, speculative

Evercore ISI's Mark Mahaney raised his Meta PT to $860 (from $820), estimating Meta could generate up to $22B in annual gross revenue starting 2027 by renting out excess AI compute capacity — Meta is reportedly doubling capacity from 7GW (2026) to ~14GW (2027), and Mahaney's math assumes commercializing just ~1GW (7% of planned capacity) at an $11B/0.5GW implied rate. He explicitly frames this as a "call option," not a base-case forecast, and contrasts Meta with sold-out cloud providers as a potential differentiated seller. Engage critically: this is a sell-side extrapolation with no confirmed Meta commercial program behind it yet (Meta's own July comments floated an AI-cloud push for excess capacity, but nothing at this specific revenue scale has been guided). Meta premarket +3.69% on the note. No thesis or prediction impact — flagging because it bears on the "genuine oversupply vs. demand-timing" question inside the computational-abundance framework (a hyperscaler having compute to rent is a different claim than the market being structurally oversupplied). Source: Benzinga/Evercore ISI, invezz.
