Overnight Developments

bull Micron FQ4: record print, FY27-28 guide extends the shortage with no end in sight

FQ4 FY26 revenue hit a record $54.23B (vs. ~$52.6B consensus), non-GAAP EPS $33.42 (vs. ~$32.56 est.). Core Data Center revenue was $18.0B (+56% QoQ, 90% gross margin); data center SSD revenue alone was ~$10B, >10x the year-ago quarter. DRAM was 73% of total revenue with ASPs up high-teens % sequentially. FQ1 FY27 guidance: $61.5B ±$1.5B revenue (vs. ~$57B consensus) and EPS $38.15 ±$1.00 — management expects sequential revenue growth every quarter of FY27. CEO Sanjay Mehrotra: "We do not have line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance," with 2027–2028 conditions expected tighter than 2026. Management also raised FY27 capex. MU traded -1.03% premarket this morning — a muted reaction to an unambiguous beat, consistent with the market discounting the capex raise rather than questioning demand. Micron 8-K (SEC) · CNBC

bear Broadcom to lend Anthropic up to $42B — the most circular financing structure logged this cycle

Anthropic's IPO prospectus discloses a Broadcom convertible-note facility of up to $42B, financing roughly a third of Anthropic's $125.2B five-year TPU-capacity lease commitment. The notes could convert into Anthropic equity; Broadcom may designate a financing partner; no conversion price, maturity, or interest-payment terms were disclosed. Anthropic's own filing warns that a lease payment or performance default could accelerate a substantial portion of its obligations immediately due — while simultaneously limiting its ability to draw on the very $42B facility meant to cover such a default. Anthropic is separately on track to become the largest customer in Broadcom's chip-design business next year. Broadcom is now lender, prospective equity holder, supplier, and top customer to the same counterparty at once. AVGO +0.23% premarket; no negative reaction yet. Reuters

watch Gemini 4 Argon ships with autonomous vulnerability-patching, but Google gates the release

Google's new frontier model (replacing Gemini 3.5 Pro) can locate, validate, and patch critical software vulnerabilities without human intervention — a genuine capability jump, not a demo. Rather than a standard developer/API launch, Google is rolling it out first to a "Fairwind Program" of trusted cyber-defenders, with a guardrail-free version planned only for vetted defenders and internal teams and no general-availability date given. This is a more pre-emptive caution posture than the post-hoc responses to OpenAI's and Meta's agent incidents logged over the past two weeks — Google itself appears to assess the vulnerability-finding capability as dual-use.
