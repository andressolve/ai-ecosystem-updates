Overnight Developments

bear Political/data-center-opposition axis escalates: Ohio joins, Meta concedes it, credit confirms it

Ohio's governor-elect Amy Acton (D) campaigned on a conditional data-center moratorium (developers must cover full utility costs, use union labor, and meet environmental standards), making her the third incoming governor — alongside Texas's Gina Hinojosa and Georgia's Keisha Lance Bottoms — elected Tuesday on an explicit data-center restriction platform. Wells Fargo's post-election sizing puts Texas (27%) + Georgia (7%) + Ohio (5%) at a combined 39% of planned US data-center power capacity, a sharper figure than yesterday's "roughly a third-plus" estimate (Yahoo Finance/Wells Fargo). Separately, unofficial tallies show local data-center moratorium or restriction ballot measures passed in 43 of 44 votes across 10 states Tuesday — the public-opinion base beneath the three governors' races (Fortune).

Meta's President and Vice Chair, Dina Powell McCormick, said on the record at the WSJ Tech conference in Napa (Wednesday) that after Tuesday's results, "to start new data center build-outs today in the United States will be very challenging," and she expects "a lot more review" of new projects — while noting some officials remain open to negotiation (Gizmodo; Seeking Alpha). This is a conference remark, not an earnings-call citation — the specific trigger the thesis file set yesterday for escalating "Very Strong" to the next level — but it is the first unambiguous, on-record concession from a Big 4 hyperscaler executive.

Credit markets moved beyond equities overnight: CoreWeave's 2031 bonds traded as low as 79.5 cents, new lows, and the stock closed Wednesday at $49.85, its lowest level since May 2025 (Bloomberg). This morning's premarket shows a further leg down: CoreWeave -3.92%, Nebius -3.88%, on top of Tuesday's -9.3%/-7.2%. Seoul's close was the sharpest reaction to this axis yet — Kospi -5.28%, SK Hynix -9.46%, Samsung -7.57% — roughly double Tuesday's -5.0%/-4.2% move on the separate (financing-fragility) Nasdaq-correction story, suggesting the market now rates political/siting risk above funding-access risk.

Why this matters: breadth (three states and a 43-of-44 ballot base, not three governors), credit confirmation (bonds, not just equity), and corporate acknowledgment (a Tier 1 executive on record) all moved in the same direction in one session. Escalated to Very Strong on the thesis tracker, up from Strong. Still short of falsification: no capacity has actually been halted, and none of the three governors takes office before January 2027. The monitoring trigger is unchanged — a binding post-inauguration agency directive, or an actual earnings-call citation by a Tier 1 hyperscaler, would move this further.

bear Micron pauses $4.5B Taiwan HBM/DRAM fab over a labor dispute

Micron has largely frozen construction of its Taoyuan, Taiwan plant — budgeted at $3-4.5B and intended to add HBM/DRAM capacity by 2028 — after the local union demanded 15% of operating profit paid fully in cash rather than stock. Micron is reportedly concerned a prior agreement could be overturned; only minimal site security continues. The company says current Taiwan operations are unaffected. MU fell -4.67% premarket, closing below $800 for the first time since July (Bloomberg; GuruFocus).

This is a sharper escalation of the Taoyuan dispute than October's scheduled one-day warning strike — a full construction freeze on a facility earmarked for future HBM/DRAM capacity, not a strike. Two-sided for the thesis: bearish for the supply-side response (one capacity-expansion project now frozen indefinitely, on top of SK Hynix's separate export-license-stalled New York plant logged yesterday), but it reinforces the pricing-power read by removing future relief. Does not change P015 (DRAM contract pricing through C3Q26, already at its 95% ceiling), which resolves on a nearer horizon than this fab's 2028 timeline.

watch NVIDIA: ~600,000 H200 chips stuck in Hong Kong awaiting Chinese import approval

Reuters reports roughly 600,000 of NVIDIA's H200 chips sit in Hong Kong awaiting Beijing's approval for import into mainland China. Several major Chinese internet firms (reportedly including Alibaba, ByteDance, and DeepSeek) have each obtained about 10,000 H200s under a limited framework agreed after July's US-China trade talks, but Beijing hasn't approved large-volume imports; total approvals could land under 200,000 chips. An 8-card H200 server reportedly sold in mainland China for over $900K by late October, roughly 3x its price a year earlier (Reuters via Yahoo).

This confirms, with a concrete volume figure, the China-demand-but-blocked dynamic already behind NVIDIA's $0 China Data Center revenue assumption and the reported 51%+ non-China capacity allocation policy on new contracts (logged 11/3). No prediction impact.

neutral Microsoft declines to expand a Nscale Texas lease — not clearly the same political story

Microsoft has declined an option that would have more than doubled its Nscale lease at the Ward County, Texas site — a deal the FT describes as worth "tens of billions of dollars" at the larger size. Microsoft's existing Texas contract is unaffected; this is a declined expansion, not a cancellation (FT via search). It follows Microsoft's October walk-away from a separate ~$3B Nscale UK lease over grid-connection delays. Nscale lost $1B on $141M of H1 2026 revenue and is preparing a roughly $35B IPO.

No reporting ties this decision to the election, and it lands the same week Nscale disclosed weak financials ahead of its own IPO — read this as Microsoft capital discipline toward a stretched counterparty, not confirmed evidence of the political axis, even though the timing is coincidental.
