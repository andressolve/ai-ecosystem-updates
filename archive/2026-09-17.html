Overnight Developments

watch Fed hikes 25bp as expected; Warsh's tone reads hawkish beyond the dot plot

The FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the federal funds rate 25bp to 3.75-4.00% (9/16, 2pm ET) — the first hike since 2023, matching the 88-91% market-implied odds flagged in the prior two reports. The Summary of Economic Projections showed 16 of 18 participants expecting at least one more hike in 2026 (4 expecting two), and Chair Warsh's press conference leaned hawkish relative to the median dot, arguing current financial conditions are "not particularly restrictive." Equity markets took it in stride: S&P 500 +0.4%, Nasdaq +0.8% on the day. Why this matters for coverage: the 9/14 report flagged rising hike odds as a new, distinct risk axis for P008 (hyperscaler capex financing) and CoreWeave's 14.3x debt/equity specifically. The hike itself has now happened — the precondition for that risk is in place — but the actual trigger (a hyperscaler or neocloud citing financing cost as a constraint on a call) has not fired. No probability change; watch Q3 earnings calls (CoreWeave, Nebius, and the Big-5 hyperscalers reporting late Oct-Nov) for the first test.

watch Huawei unveils Ascend 960DT/960PR chip roadmap days ahead of Trump-Xi summit

Huawei announced its Atlas 960 SuperPoD computing cluster and a two-chip Ascend roadmap — the 960DT shipping Q1 2027, the 960PR in Q3 2027 — at its annual conference in Shanghai (9/17), timed one week ahead of Xi Jinping's Sept 23-25 Washington visit and the Sept 24 Trump-Xi meeting. Huawei framed this explicitly as narrowing the gap with NVIDIA. Most independent technical assessments still place Huawei's Ascend line one to two generations behind NVIDIA/TSMC's leading edge on both process node and packaging, so this is a competitive-trajectory data point (who captures value in China's domestic stack) rather than a near-term demand or share-shift signal for NVIDIA. The timing — a show of technological self-sufficiency days before a head-of-state meeting where AI chip export policy is on the agenda — is itself informative about China's negotiating posture.

neutral US chip-industry workforce shortfall threatens fab buildout timelines

CNBC (9/17), citing a SEMI Foundation/McKinsey analysis, reports the US semiconductor industry faces a projected shortfall of 127,000-157,000 workers by 2030 as TSMC ($265B, Arizona), Micron ($100B, New York), and Samsung (Texas) scale up domestic manufacturing. Samsung and SK Hynix executives told CNBC they are temporarily rotating in Korea-based staff to launch US lines; Samsung's Austin semiconductor VP described struggling to find qualified candidates locally. This is a genuine, quantified execution-risk data point on the physical/infrastructure layer — distinct from (and additive to) the memory/HBM supply-side constraints already tracked under P015/P028/P029 — but it is a pace/timing risk to guided capacity ramps, not a change to underlying demand.
