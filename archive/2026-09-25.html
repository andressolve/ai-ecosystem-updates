Overnight Developments

bear Oracle sends force majeure notice on $165B Project Jupiter; gas pipeline slipped ~6 months

Oracle sent a force majeure notice to Stack Infrastructure (Blue Owl Capital unit), developer of the 2.45 GW New Mexico Stargate campus, per Bloomberg as relayed by CNBC and TechCrunch (direct CNBC fetch returned 403; details are from search summaries). The trigger is power: Energy Transfer's 17-mile "Green Chile" pipeline, meant to fuel the Bloom Energy fuel cells, moved from ~August 2026 to Feb 1, 2027 after repeated New Mexico State Land Office right-of-way denials, and a separate air-quality permit has a Nov 23 state decision deadline. Oracle is reportedly not exiting as anchor tenant; if both sides agree a force majeure event occurred, Oracle could defer rent up to three years should the campus miss its 2028 online target. Reported reaction: ORCL about -3-4%, Blue Owl -4%, Bloom Energy -6%.

What it changes: this is a supply/timeline problem, not demand destruction (OpenAI's capacity need is unchanged; the site is simply not powerable on schedule). It matters for the thesis in two ways. (1) The "physical layer" constraint is now visible in a flagship Stargate site, which is what the constraints-first framework predicts and which caps how fast demand can convert into installed compute. (2) The financing chain is the weak link: a tenant deferring rent shifts risk to Blue Owl/Stack and their lenders (sub-thesis #5, informal financing-fragility label). Related, same theme: New Jersey fined DataOne (Nebius' Vineland site for Microsoft) $1.07M for 62 unpermitted 1.98 MW gas generators and set a 45-day compliance deadline, but it can keep running during review (NJ Monitor; Tom's Hardware says the site is tied to a $33B deal, other outlets say $17B, unresolved). Bull rebuttal: delays are 2028-dated, other Stargate sites (Abilene etc.) are live, and NVDA/Colossus deployments below show compute is still landing where power exists. Guess (not sourced): the market is pricing this as Oracle-specific, not stack-wide.

[bull/watch] Anthropic signs $11.6B, 7-year CPU-capacity agreement with Akamai; warrant gives Anthropic up to ~5% of Akamai

Akamai press release (9/24, after close): $11.6B over seven years (~$1.7B/yr average), an option for another ~$9B (~$20B total), workload described as Anthropic's "accelerating CPU workload demands." Akamai estimates ~$5.5B of related capex (~47% of the commitment) and ~$1.7B of extra 2026 capex for supply-chain components including memory; no change to 2026 revenue guidance. Anthropic gets a warrant for non-voting Series B preferred convertible into 7.7M shares (~5%) at $111.33, ~2% vesting now and ~1% per additional $3B of services. AKAM +23% premarket per Invezz; BofA, Guggenheim, Piper, JPM and UBS raised targets (BofA also flagged read-through to CPU demand for AMD).

Why it matters: agent harnesses, sandboxes and tool execution run on CPUs, which is direct evidence for sub-thesis #1 (agentic demand is additive and reaches beyond accelerators) and adds a memory-demand leg at Akamai. Weakest links: (1) circularity: the customer receives equity in the supplier, the same structure as the NVDA/AMD/MediaTek warrant and investment deals already logged; (2) revenue is back-loaded and unguided (no 2026 impact); (3) the contract size is a commitment, and Anthropic's own funding depends on the IPO/market window (see next item). I can't decompose how much of the $11.6B is GPU-adjacent versus general CPU: the release doesn't say.

bull Claude finds a novel CRISPR-like enzyme system (ART) in 21 hours: capability real, function unproven

Anthropic reported (9/23, primary post) that ~950 Claude agents used ~210M tokens over 21 hours to screen >200,000 reverse transcriptases and flag "array-associated reverse transcriptases" (ART): an RT, an accessory protein and a CRISPR-like repeat array found mainly in bacteriophages. Human scientists validated by protein expression and biochemistry that the arrays are expressed as distinct short RNAs. Function is unknown and no gene-editing activity is shown. Feng Zhang (Broad) called it "exciting" and worth more investigation.

Thesis read (sub-thesis #3, AI-for-science): a genuine example of a search strategy that would not have been feasible at scarce-compute prices, but a weak demand signal. 210M tokens is a small compute bill (my guess: low-thousands of dollars at API pricing); the binding cost is wet-lab validation. The abundance thesis needs science applications that consume compute at data-center scale, not screening runs that cost less than one lab technician-week. The bar for real support is a validated function or a commercial product (sub-thesis #3 falsification: commercial product traced to AI-led discovery by end-2027). Anthropic is grading its own result; treat as Weak-Moderate.

[bull/watch] Meta's Muse: JPMorgan raises PT to $920 on early adoption; Meta near 52-week high

JPMorgan (Anmuth) lifted META to $920 from $820, calling Muse the potential "most widely used consumer AI application since ChatGPT"; cited data: 2.8M US/Canada downloads in 12 days and 1.43M iOS downloads vs 1.37M for ChatGPT over the same window (Benzinga, CNBC via search; direct fetch blocked). Meta is up roughly 30% in September per the same coverage. What it changes: it is the first consumer-scale agent (transacts, fills forms, books travel) with early adoption evidence, which supports sub-thesis #1 on the consumer side. Weakest links: downloads are not retention or paid engagement; iOS-only comparisons omit ChatGPT's much smaller 2022 install base; Amazon has been blocking Muse from Amazon.com since 9/20 (logged), which caps the agent's most valuable use case; and JPM's number is a sell-side extrapolation. Muse's inference load is not disclosed.
