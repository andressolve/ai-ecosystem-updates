Overnight Developments

bull Broadcom CEO reaffirms unchanged AI-semi targets; Trump calls AI-safety fears a "hoax" in live Huang call — the demand side pushes back on the Amodei panic

Broadcom CEO Hock Tan told CNBC (9/14) that the company's AI-semiconductor revenue targets are unchanged: $115B FY2027 and $230B FY2028 (both first disclosed on the 9/2 Q3 FY2026 call), despite the prior day's broadest AI-infrastructure selloff this cycle triggered by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay. Tan added that Anthropic — the lab whose CEO's essay caused the panic — remains on track to become Broadcom's largest custom-chip customer in 2027 and to hold that position through 2028. Broadcom fell 4.8% Monday anyway (iShares Semiconductor ETF -5.6%) on the rhetoric-driven selloff. Separately, NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang was on stage at the All-In Summit in Los Angeles when President Trump called him and was put on speakerphone for roughly five minutes, captured on video and circulated widely. "I'm telling you, it's all a hoax," Trump said of AI-existential-risk warnings, adding those opposing data-center construction are "playing right into the hands of a lot of people that don't want to see it happen" — a reprise of his 9/13 rejection of any US slowdown, now delivered live alongside NVIDIA's CEO. Why this is the sharpest test yet: Tan had every incentive to hedge language if he saw early signs of demand softening in AI semiconductors specifically — he did the opposite, and the detail that Anthropic itself is simultaneously increasing its committed spend with Broadcom while its CEO calls for a capability slowdown is a clean, on-record contradiction between rhetoric and revealed behavior. Logged as Strong supporting evidence to theses/computational-abundance.md (vs. the 9/14 TSMC capacity-roadmap entry's Moderate, since this is a direct company statement rather than secondary reporting on an internal roadmap). Congress remains unmoved either direction — GOP leadership continues rejecting AI regulation (The Hill, AP, 9/14) while some members push for guardrails (The Guardian, 9/15) — no legislative action pending.

watch Microsoft publishes 37-page "Humanist AI Code of Conduct" — Nadella's concrete follow-through on "deliberate pacing," but governance, not a compute commitment

Microsoft published a draft "Humanist AI Code of Conduct" (9/14), five to six months in development, opening a six-week comment period before a final version publishes by year-end. Core principle: "humans matter more than AI" — Microsoft's models will operate only within human-approved scopes, cannot independently set goals or exceed granted authority, must never resist correction or shutdown, and must communicate in human-understandable ways. This is Satya Nadella's concrete follow-through on the "deliberate pacing" position he staked out 9/13 in response to Amodei's essay — but it is a governance/behavioral-constraint framework for Microsoft's own AI (MAI) models, not a commitment to reduce training runs, GPU orders, or data-center capex. Distinguishing this cleanly matters: "pacing capability behavior" and "reducing compute demand" remain different claims, consistent with how P037 is framed. Not logged to the thesis Supporting/Disconfirming trackers — it's adjacent color on the same week's rhetoric, not a demand-side datapoint in either direction.

watch TSMC confirms no High-NA EUV adoption through 2029 — an ASML-specific growth-timing risk, not a capacity signal

Reports (Bernstein note, multiple outlets, 9/14) confirm TSMC has no current plans to adopt ASML's High-NA EUV lithography tools through 2029: its next-gen A13 node (2029 production start) will use conventional (low-NA) EUV multi-patterning instead. Baseline High-NA adoption expectation pushes to the A10 node, ~2030. ASML fell as much as ~5-7% intraday Monday on the news, compounding that day's broader selloff; the live Amsterdam quote has since recovered to +1.53% this morning in the relief bounce. This complicates the timing of ASML's highest-value product line (High-NA machines list near ~$400M vs. ~$200M for standard EUV) but is not a capacity or demand-side signal — TSMC relying on cheaper conventional-EUV multi-patterning for longer is consistent with confidence in high wafer volumes on existing tools, not caution about AI-driven demand. Not logged to the computational abundance tracker; flagged in watchlist/tier2/asml.md as an ASML-specific risk.

watch Nvidia, Palantir, Booz Allen restrict Anthropic model usage over data-retention concerns

The Information (9/14, via Reuters) reported that Nvidia, Palantir, and Booz Allen Hamilton have curtailed use of Anthropic's commercial models over IP/data-retention fears, traced to a June 2026 Anthropic policy change permitting 30-day usage-log retention for "complex and novel attack" defense. Palantir is pressing for irrevocable zero-data-retention guarantees before broader integration; Nvidia limits Anthropic use to less-sensitive tasks (using its own Nemotron models internally instead); Booz Allen has barred Anthropic's commercial model for proprietary cybersecurity work. Microsoft is reportedly capitalizing on the unease by pitching isolated cloud environments and its own AI offerings. This is customer-trust friction specific to Anthropic's enterprise business — a different mechanism from the Amodei compute-demand debate happening the same week, but worth tracking given Anthropic's centrality to this cycle's custom-silicon and hyperscaler-capex threads. Logged in watchlist/tier3/anthropic.md; not added to the thesis tracker (single-lab enterprise-trust issue, not an aggregate demand signal).
