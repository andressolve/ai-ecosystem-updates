Overnight Developments

bear Broadcom's $60B AI-chip debt facility crystallizes into a named $42B/$18B tranche structure

Bloomberg (10/2) reports Broadcom is in talks with a lender group to raise more than $60B in debt to fund AI-chip infrastructure for Anthropic and other customers. Reported structure: a $42B Class A senior-secured tranche syndicated among banks, plus an $18B Class B junior tranche led by Blackstone — Blackstone is putting up $9B of its own capital and plans to syndicate the rest. This is the next visible step in an architecture this file has tracked since 8/21 (when the mandate was first reported "in talks," size vague, $60-100B range) and 10/1 (when Anthropic's IPO prospectus disclosed the $42B Broadcom lending facility specifically). The new information is the capital stack itself: private credit (Blackstone) is confirmed taking the riskier junior slice, on top of the bank-syndicated senior tranche. Nothing is finalized. AVGO +1.97% premarket — but that move landed inside a broad market rally driven by the weak jobs print (see below), not isolated to this news. Bloomberg · qz.com

bear Amazon reportedly explores $8B Nvidia-chip sale-leaseback SPV — first Big 5 hyperscaler to try this structure

The Financial Times (via Reuters, 10/2) reports Amazon has held talks about moving roughly $8B of Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips — already installed across 12+ US data centers in five states, including Nevada and Virginia — into a special-purpose vehicle. The SPV would issue debt to outside investors, who would also be offered up to a 10% equity stake, while Amazon leases the same hardware back to keep running it. Nothing is agreed or signed; neither company commented. This matters less for what it says about Amazon's balance sheet strength (it remains the strongest of the Big 5 — self-funded capex, a record 2026 bond issuance at tight spreads) and more for what it implies about the industry: if even the hyperscaler with the least need for creative financing is shopping deployed, revenue-generating GPUs into an SPV, the accounting/credit-rating incentive to keep AI capex off top-of-house balance sheets looks structural across the industry rather than a workaround unique to sub-investment-grade borrowers like CoreWeave or Anthropic.
