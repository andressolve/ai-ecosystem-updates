Overnight Developments

bear NVIDIA pauses revenue-sharing deals with AI cloud partners on antitrust concerns

WSJ (8/27, via Reuters/Yahoo Finance, sourced to people familiar) reports NVIDIA paused parts of the vendor-financing structure it disclosed in its own Q2 FY27 10-Q (quarter ended 7/26/26): six-year committed compute agreements with select AI cloud partners totaling $36B in commitments, with NVIDIA earning a 50% revenue share above a threshold. The pause reportedly happened around the week of 8/20-8/21, driven by internal antitrust concerns and friction over NVIDIA's control of partner business practices — including which end-customers partners could serve and a stated preference for spreading capacity across smaller firms rather than concentrating it. NVIDIA's on-record statement: "The new business model we introduced in July that opens up compute access to the fast-growing AI ecosystem is still in place and continues to evolve due to high demand." No primary source names specific paused counterparties; secondary coverage speculating about CoreWeave, Nebius, or IREN exposure is not primary-confirmed, so none of those files were updated on this story.

This is the first time NVIDIA has pulled back — even partially — from a demand-capture mechanism it created itself, rather than simply expanding it (the pattern all cycle has been MOUs, guarantees, and financing platforms scaling up). It reads as a governance/antitrust-risk pause on program structure, not a demand pullback: the underlying $36B compute-commitment book is unaffected per NVIDIA's own statement, and no partner has said it needs less compute. Logged Moderate disconfirming to theses/computational-abundance.md (sub-thesis #5, financing fragility/value-capture). No prediction impact.

bull Marvell beats and raises FY28 to ~$18B — stock falls anyway on Google-deal timing

Marvell reported Q2 FY2027 (8/27 AMC): record revenue $2.739B (+37% YoY), data center revenue $2.17B (+46% YoY), and raised its FY2028 revenue outlook to ~$18B (from $16.5B). The stock fell ~7.9-8% premarket despite the beat-and-raise. The reason: nearly all shares under the Google attach-silicon warrant deal (a ~$120B cumulative purchase ceiling through FY2033, logged here 8/20) vest in tranches counted starting August 1, 2026 — the last day of the just-reported quarter — so the revenue-recognition clock lies almost entirely ahead. CEO Matt Murphy said Google-related revenue "would contribute much more significantly in FY2029." Reuters confirms the sell-off is specifically a timing/expectations story around the Google deal, not a demand miss.

This resolves open item #16 (watch the 8/27 print for deal-economics commentary). It confirms the underlying demand signal is real — record data center revenue, a raised guide — but the market reaction confirms it had run ahead of near-term recognition on the Google deal specifically. Consistent with the "world vs. market" pattern seen repeatedly this cycle (AMAT 8/14, multiple TSMC prints). No sign yet that Marvell's Google content is creeping from attach silicon toward the TPU compute die itself — the more thesis-relevant escalation this tracker watches for remains open. Logged Strong supporting to theses/computational-abundance.md (sub-thesis #4, custom silicon).

Reuters (8/27, single-sourced) reports Anthropic discussed acquiring AI chip startup MatX for roughly $7B, then abandoned the deal in favor of pursuing a partnership/customer relationship instead. No on-record confirmation from Anthropic or MatX.

Two-sided read: an abandoned silicon-startup acquisition is mildly consistent with Anthropic staying compute-vendor-diversified (NVIDIA + Google TPU + AWS Trainium) rather than becoming a silicon owner via M&A — modest support for P003 holding at 38% (Anthropic NVIDIA-only framing) — but it doesn't bear on the actual training-compute-mix question P003 resolves on, and a customer/partnership relationship with a chip startup is itself a step toward diversification, just a lighter-weight one than acquisition. Logged Moderate, two-sided to theses/computational-abundance.md (sub-thesis #4). Context note added to P003 in predictions/prediction-log.md; no probability change.

bull SK Hynix breaks ground on first US facility (Indiana)

SK Hynix held a groundbreaking ceremony (primary source: news.skhynix.com, 8/27) for its first US production facility — an advanced-packaging and R&D center (not a wafer fab) at Purdue Research Park, West Lafayette, Indiana, representing a >$4B investment. Cleanroom operations are targeted for H2 2028, with HBM4E volume production targeted for Q3 2029. CEO Kwak Noh-Jung called it a "key HBM production base in America" by 2030 and reaffirmed — in softer form than his original July 2026 Nasdaq-listing remarks — that memory supply-demand would "become more balanced" only after 2030 (Korea Herald, CNBC).

Moderate supporting evidence for the structural memory-undersupply thesis (cross-refs P015/P028/P029), but the 2028-2029 timeline means it doesn't move the near-term (through 2027-28) supply picture those predictions are actually keyed to. Logged to theses/computational-abundance.md, watchlist/tier2/sk-hynix.md.
