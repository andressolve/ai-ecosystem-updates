Overnight Developments

bull TSMC evaluates second US campus in Texas; Piper Sandler says leading-edge capacity sold out through 1H 2028

Reuters (sources, 9/30) reports TSMC is evaluating a major second US site, most likely in Dallas, planning up to six additional advanced fabs. Because the new fabs would target more advanced nodes than the initial Arizona buildout, total investment could exceed the $265B already committed there. The plan is not yet board-approved and TSMC did not comment; reporting frames it as customer-demand-driven, with NVIDIA, Intel, AMD, and Apple all cited as pushing for more US-based advanced production (Reuters via US News, BigGo).

Separately, a Taiwan-semiconductor-research expert speaking at a Piper Sandler webinar (reported 9/30) said TSMC's leading-edge wafer capacity is sold out through 1H 2028, with XPU (GPU+ASIC) unit shipments projected to grow 50% in 2027 and a further 40% in 2028 (~66% of that growth ASIC-driven, ~33% GPU-driven) — though the same source flagged tool-supplier capex struggling to keep pace, and named memory (HBM/DRAM/flash) shortages as "the biggest system-level risk" to next-gen system design (Investing.com).

Why it matters: a foundry evaluating >$265B of incremental US capex ahead of confirmed multi-year demand, on top of a sell-side view that supply is already sold out ~15 months forward, is direct physical-layer confirmation that demand is running ahead of the industry's own planning horizon — the core claim of the computational-abundance thesis. Weakest link: the Texas plan is pre-board-approval and TSMC gave no comment; treat as a reported evaluation, not committed capex, pending TSMC's own confirmation (next opportunity: ~mid-October Q3 call). Logged in theses/computational-abundance.md Supporting Evidence tracker and watchlist/tier1/TSM.md.

watch Trump's AI-risk lunch ends in voluntary "self-regulation" accord, not binding rules

Trump hosted ~20 AI/tech executives at a White House lunch (9/29) — the direct policy response to the week's agent-safety incidents (OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra cancellation, Meta's Muse home-address incident, the 9/26 training pause). Attendees included NVIDIA's Huang, Anthropic's Amodei, Meta's Zuckerberg, Alphabet's Pichai, Microsoft's Nadella, Amazon's Bezos, AMD's Su, and OpenAI's Brockman. Outcome: the group signed a "statement of principles" on "superintelligence" — Speaker Johnson called it a "joint commitment" to four layers of controls and audits (internal evaluations, external-firm audits, board review). Asked if it was binding, Trump said "I think it's morally binding," while also saying there was agreement in the room on "tremendous self-regulation," continuing to reject new federal AI safety regulation (CBS News, CNN, NBC News).

Why it matters: this is genuinely two-sided. Bull-adjacent (for continued capex): a voluntary, non-enforceable pledge — explicitly framed by the President as self-regulation, not new law — removes the nearest-term path to a regulatory capex constraint, reinforcing P037's 92% hold. Bear-adjacent (for the safety debate): that the policy response to a week of concrete production-agent failures was a pledge with no third-party enforcement mechanism is exactly the "self-regulation is inadequate" critique the bear case raises. No probability change — P037 resolves on a disclosed capex/compute cut, not the regulatory environment. Logged as a context note on P037 and in the thesis disconfirming/two-sided tracker.

neutral DeepSeek open-sources Huawei-Ascend programming tools, reducing CUDA dependence — China-confined

DeepSeek open-sourced TileLang, a high-level programming language for Huawei's Ascend AI chips (positioned as simpler than NVIDIA's CUDA), plus related compute/communication libraries, developed jointly with Huawei. DeepSeek's V4-Flash model is now optimized specifically for Ascend, and the company plans to install 160,000+ Ascend chips at a new Inner Mongolia data center; the two firms also advanced a "supernode" design based on 128 Ascend 950 chips (Reuters via Business Recorder, NYT).

Why it matters: a concrete, quantified step in a China-domestic-stack trend that's structurally forced by US export controls rather than a new threat to NVIDIA's actual (ex-China) revenue base — NVIDIA already can't sell advanced chips into China. Matters for the long-run "CUDA moat forever" assumption if Chinese tooling or chips eventually compete outside China, but the near-term read-through is limited. No prediction impact.

watch Meta's AI-datacenter tax strategy draws Senate scrutiny

The New York Times reported Meta has claimed billions in federal research tax credits by classifying its AI data centers as "experimental" facilities that could fail — a distinction the company's own accountants reportedly flagged as legally risky. The savings: $700M (2023) → $2.0B (2024) → $3.9B (2025); Meta's total federal tax expense fell from $9.6B (2024) to $2.8B (2025). The Hyperion data center (Richland Parish, LA) expanded to 5GW at a cost of >$50B under this framing. Senator Elizabeth Warren and other lawmakers sent formal inquiries to Meta and several other large tech companies on 9/28 demanding answers on AI/data-center tax deductions (NYT via Quinta's weblog summary, Quartz).

Why it matters: this is a previously-invisible subsidy layer inside the capex buildout — if the R&D tax-credit classification holds, hyperscaler AI capex is materially cheaper on an after-tax basis than headline figures suggest, a mild positive for buildout economics (sub-thesis #5 economics, not financing structure). The offsetting risk is now concrete: a sitting senator's formal inquiry is the first direct legislative-scrutiny vector on this specific mechanism, and if closed by rule change or statute, could raise the effective cost of the buildout for Meta and any peer using a similar classification. Not yet a thesis-moving event — no legislative or regulatory action has occurred — but worth tracking given the dollar amounts involved.
